## Key question 1: What window or cell size do site-specific weed management (SSWM) studies use for spray decisions, and why?

### Takeaway
Published UAV weed-mapping studies decide on cells of 0.5 x 0.5 m up to 3 x 3 m, and the size is always set to match the sprayer (nozzle spacing, part-width section, or crop row), never to the plant. The published cover thresholds (5 %, 10 %, 15 %) were derived from field-plot or whole-field trials and are applied in the cited UAV work either to 1 m-scale cells or to the whole orchard mean; nobody applies a 10-15 % cover threshold to a 0.25 m2 cell. Paice's long-term economics for blackgrass set an upper bound of about 6 x 6 m on the control unit.

### Cited findings
- Peña et al. 2013 (maize, Spain, 2 cm GSD from 30 m): the grid "length used was 1 m and the grid width used was the inter-row distance (0.7 m on average)"; cells 1 x 0.68-0.75 m. Weed cover per cell = "the ratio of weed pixels to total pixels per grid". Classes were weed-free, low (<5 %), moderate (5-20 %), high (>20 %); the authors call this categorisation an example (authors' choice). Field: 23 % weed-free, 47 % low, 23 % moderate, 7 % high; cells with any weeds = 77 % of 14,000 m2. Grid dimensions "can be customized on the basis of cropping patterns and the specifications required by the herbicide spraying machinery"; no sprayer-based rationale for the 1 m is given. Estimated vs observed cover R2 = 0.89, RMSE 0.02; 3-class map 86 % overall accuracy, kappa 0.76. — [Peña et al. 2013, PLoS ONE (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3795646)
- López-Granados et al. 2016 (sunflower, Precision Agriculture 17:183-199): grid-based weed-cover maps; "a weed threshold can be derived, which is the percentage of weed cover in every grid, above which a treatment is required"; seven thresholds "with 2.5 steps of increasing ratio from 0 % ... to 15 %" (authors' choice). Example: field 2, 30 m altitude, multispectral camera, "a range of 23-3 % of the field (i.e., 77 and 97 % of area) could be treated for 0-15 % weed thresholds" (snippet wording as found; the 77/97 % figure reads as untreated area). Visible camera accuracy only 50-60 % at the 0 % threshold. The grid cell size could not be retrieved (paywalled). — [Springer record](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s11119-015-9415-8); [Crossref metadata](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1007/s11119-015-9415-8)
- Castaldi et al. 2017 (silage maize, 4 fields, Central Italy): herbicide "saving ... calculated in terms of untreated area ranged between 14 and 39.2 %" vs blanket application, with no yield penalty; maps were prescription maps checked against ground truth. Cell size not in the abstract (full text paywalled). — [CNR IRIS record](https://iris.cnr.it/handle/20.500.14243/327815); [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1007/s11119-016-9468-3)
- Huang et al. 2018 (rice, Sensors; 0.5 cm/pixel at 10 m): prescription grid "0.5 x 0.5 m", chosen "in accordance with the site-specific sprayer" and "adjustable according to the different spraying machines" (authors' choice). A cell is treated if its weed coverage exceeds the threshold; thresholds 0.00-0.25 in 0.05 steps gave herbicide savings 58.3 %, 64.1 %, 66.4 %, 68.1 %, 69.6 %, 70.8 %. They recommend threshold 0.0 "because it minimizes the risk of missed weeds". No filtering or smoothing of the pixel map before gridding. — [Huang et al. 2018 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6209949)
- NDSU corn trial (arXiv 2206.01734; GSD 0.63 cm from 350 ft): first "a grid of cells of 10 x 10 ft was overlaid ... The idea behind using these grid cells was to provide a buffer area for the spraying (if there is at least one weed that is completely within the cell, the whole cell would be sprayed)"; because weed pressure was high this "would result in spraying almost 100% of the cells", so they switched to "1.67 (distance between nozzles) x 10 ft", i.e. 0.5 m wide cells matching nozzle spacing but narrower than the 0.7 m corn rows; on average 35 % of cells unsprayed. Corn rows were buffered 3.5 inches each side and removed before counting weeds. Post-harvest, SSWC plots had more weeds than broadcast plots; the authors say the 1.6 x 10 ft cells "could have reduced the effectiveness of the entire research approach" and that "approximately 10 x 10 ft cell size would have been useful for better efficacy". Sprayer ran at 6.5 mph with 10 Hz RTK so position resolution was "at least one foot per 1/10th of a second". — [arXiv 2206.01734](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.01734)
- Montull et al. 2024 (maize, Revista de Ciências Agrárias): OBIA weed map from UAV, doses set "on a 1 m2 grid using a treatment threshold of 1 plant/m2", savings "above 38 %" (search snippet; PDF returned 503). — [Montull et al. 2024](https://revistas.rcaap.pt/rca/article/download/34975/24652/157327)
- Paice et al. 1998 (stochastic simulation, Alopecurus myosuroides): herbicide applied on/off "in square areas whose size was set by the sprayer's spatial resolution"; areas treated only "where mean density was at least one plant per m2" (authors' choice); conclusion: "patch spraying is unlikely to be profitable in the long term if the treated control area exceeds 6 m x 6 m", with higher resolution sometimes needed. Aggregation measured with Lloyd's patchiness index, negative binomial k, and a Fourier-transform estimate of the spatial scale of aggregation. — [Rothamsted repository record](https://repository.rothamsted.ac.uk/item/87y3q/a-stochastic-simulation-model-for-evaluating-the-concept-of-patch-spraying)
- Commercial sprayer geometry: "the classic part-width section often is 3 to 5 m wide"; electric individual nozzle control gives actual 50 cm part-width sections. — [Amazone](https://amazone.net/en/products-digital-solutions/electric-individual-nozzle-control-with-actual-50-cm-part-width-section-control-56820)
- Villette et al. 2019 (INRAE simulation): the benefit of narrower sections "depends on weed spatial distribution and aggregation degree"; "for uniformly dispersed small patches and the use of narrow sections (1 to 4 nozzles) equipped with triangular spray pattern nozzles, under-dosing areas can increase" (search snippet; HAL page bot-blocked). — [HAL record](https://hal.inrae.fr/hal-02609786)
- Boom-width simulation in spring cereals: "for boom widths of 15 m and 24 m, the spraying errors were about 10% and 15%", while "simulated herbicide reductions were not influenced by boom width"; same source reports average potential reductions of 60 %, 64 % and 53 % for original, +25 % and -25 % thresholds (search snippet, host DeepDyve, original authorship not confirmed). — [DeepDyve record](https://www.deepdyve.com/lp/doc/aCbgeGK6dG)
- Cotton patch-spray study: "the overall error was usually reduced as boom resolution increased; however, increased levels of Type OS errors [areas needing spray that were missed] were detected at some narrower boom widths". — [Beltwide Cotton 2005](https://www.cotton.org/beltwide/proceedings/2005-2022/data/conferences/2005/pdfs/2851.pdf)
- Allmendinger et al. 2024 (4 maize fields, SW Germany, offline UAV maps): "On average, 94% of all weed plants were correctly identified in the UAV images with the automatic classifier"; spot spraying up to 86 % weed control efficacy, equal to broadcast; early spot spraying saved 47 % herbicide; yields equal. Grid/buffer geometry not retrievable (MDPI and Hohenheim pages 403/refused). — [Europe PMC abstract](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.3390/plants13152164&resultType=core&format=json); [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2223-7747/13/15/2164)
- Gerhards & Christensen 2003 (4-year map-based patch spraying): herbicide cut "in winter cereals by 60% for herbicides against broad-leaved weeds and 90% for grass weed herbicides"; maize 78 % grass / 11 % broadleaf; sugar beet 36 % / 41 %. — [Hohenheim record](https://phytomedizin.uni-hohenheim.de/en/organization/publication/real-time-weed-detection-decision-making-and-patch-spraying-in-maize-sugar-beet-winter-wheat-and-winter-barley)
- Gerhards et al. 1997 winter wheat: broadleaf density "exceeded 40 weed seedlings m-2 in both fields", treatment recommended for 55-58 % of area, 21 % less herbicide. — [Penn State record](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/site-specific-weed-control-in-winter-wheat/)
- Turkish olive IPM guide (TAGEM 2022), weed sampling: frames of "1/4 m2 (50x50 cm)", 10 frames for 1-5 da up to 30 frames for >50 da, thrown randomly along the diagonals 10-15 m in from the edge; density per species in plants/m2 or cover % recorded; decision rule: "bahçede m2'de 10-15 veya daha fazla sayıda yabancı ot bulunuyor ya da bahçenin %10-15'i yabancı ot ile kaplı ise mücadeleye karar verilir" (treat when the orchard has 10-15 or more weeds per m2, or 10-15 % of the orchard is covered). The threshold is applied to the orchard-level sample, not per frame. — [Zeytin Entegre Mücadele Teknik Talimatı, pp. 14-15](https://www.tarimorman.gov.tr/TAGEM/Belgeler/Entegre/Zeytin%20Entegre%20M%C3%BCcadele%20Teknik%20Talimat%C4%B1.pdf)
- Keller et al. 2014 (122 winter cereal trials, SW Germany): economic thresholds "ranged from 9.2 to 9.8 % and 4.5 to 8.9 % absolute weed coverage for wheat and barley", derived at trial-plot scale, not sub-metre cells (search snippet; Springer page redirects). — [Keller et al. 2014, Gesunde Pflanzen](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10343-014-0335-8)

### Inferences
- The 0.5 m cell in Huang and the NDSU trial exists only because the sprayer nozzle is 0.5 m apart; both studies then paired it with a decision rule that is essentially "any weed" (Huang recommends threshold 0; NDSU sprays a cell containing one whole weed). Neither applies a percentage-cover threshold at 0.5 m, which is exactly the combination that makes every plant light its own cell.
- Where a cover-percentage threshold is used (Peña 5/20 %, López-Granados 2.5-15 %, Keller 9-10 %, TAGEM 10-15 %), the unit is 1 m x row-width or larger, or the whole-orchard mean. The 10-15 % orchard rule in the Turkish guide is explicitly a mean over 10-30 quadrats, so an orchard that is 15 % covered in two patches and 0 % elsewhere would still be "treat" at orchard level but the drone map should show the two patches.
- A defensible design is therefore two-level: detect at native resolution, decide at a unit the operator can act on (nozzle-section width across the row, 1-3 m along it; 6 x 6 m is the published upper bound for long-term profitability for a grass weed).

### Gaps
- The grid size used by López-Granados 2016 and Castaldi 2017 could not be read (Springer paywall, CSIC server refused). The scan-era literature usually cites 0.5 x 0.5 m to 1 x 1 m for these, but that is unverified here.
- Gerhards 2022 review (Weed Research 62:123-133, doi 10.1111/wre.12526) is embargoed at Hohenheim; only its abstract was read, so no cell-size recommendation from the review is cited.
- No source was found that justifies a cell size from patch-size statistics in a UAV orchard context; all orchard numbers above are row-crop or simulation.

## Key question 2: Is there published guidance on smoothing or aggregating fine-resolution weed cover before applying a threshold?

### Takeaway
There is no UAV weed-mapping paper found that applies kernel density estimation or an explicit minimum patch area before thresholding; the published analogues are (a) grid cells used deliberately as spray buffers, (b) interpolation (IDW/kriging) of sampled counts with a documented smoothing side-effect, (c) patchiness indices and Fourier scale of aggregation to choose the control resolution, and (d) spray-buffer zones around detections to absorb detection and positioning error. The modifiable areal unit problem is documented in general GIS literature but no weed-specific MAUP study surfaced.

### Cited findings
- Cell as buffer: NDSU "the idea behind using these grid cells was to provide a buffer area for the spraying (if there is at least one weed that is completely within the cell, the whole cell would be sprayed)". — [arXiv 2206.01734](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.01734)
- Interpolator comparison (Nebraska): "no single interpolator appears to be more precise than another"; IDW with power 4 gave lowest MAD/RMS; "for low-infestation species, the level of precision of all four interpolation methods was very poor". — [How good is your weed map? Weed Science 2003](https://complete.bioone.org/journals/weed-science/volume-51/issue-1/0043-1745(2002)051[0044:HGIYWM]2.0.CO;2/How-good-is-your-weed-map-A-comparison-of-spatial/10.1614/0043-1745(2002)051[0044:HGIYWM]2.0.CO;2.full)
- Kriging smooths patches away: "using the Gaussian copula kriging did not result in a reduction of the smoothing effect, which still represents a challenge when employing spatial interpolation methods for SSWM". — [openagrar record](https://www.openagrar.de/receive/openagrar_mods_00106258)
- Sampling density for count maps: "more than 300 random samples were required to estimate densities within 20% of the all-point means about 60% of the time"; kriged maps "could be used to establish management zones". — [Sampling to make maps for SSWM, Weed Science](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/weed-science/article/abs/sampling-to-make-maps-for-sitespecific-weed-management/7987410D19CC8F8329278AB4E3CEF44C)
- Scale-of-aggregation tools: Paice 1998 used Lloyd's patchiness index, negative binomial k, and "a Fourier-transform method for estimating the spatial scale of aggregation" to relate patch scale to sprayer resolution. — [Rothamsted record](https://repository.rothamsted.ac.uk/item/87y3q/a-stochastic-simulation-model-for-evaluating-the-concept-of-patch-spraying)
- Wallinga, Groeneveld & Lotz 1998, "Measures that describe weed spatial patterns at different levels of resolution and their applications for patch spraying of weeds", Weed Research 38(5):351. Content not retrievable; title confirms the resolution-dependence framing. — [Weed Research 1998 index](https://wiki.pestinfo.org/wiki/Weed_Research_(1998))
- Spray buffers vs detection error (Salinas, Flower, Ashworth, Renton 2026, Pest Manag Sci): a spatially explicit simulation found buffer zones around detected weeds reduce the share of weeds escaping treatment; "more accurate spraying removed susceptible weeds better but selected more strongly for resistance"; less accurate detection "kept more susceptible genotypes, and slowed resistance evolution". No buffer widths in the abstract. — [CiteDrive record of doi 10.1002/ps.71330](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/do-spatially-targeted-weed-management-strategies-increase-risk-of-herbicide-resistance-evolution/)
- MAUP (general GIS): two components, the scale problem (inferences change with aggregation size) and the zoning problem (boundary placement); "extreme values are often less common if the aggregation regions are bigger, because extreme values are averaged with lower values"; recommended practice is sensitivity testing across cell sizes and offsets. — [UCGIS GIS&T Body of Knowledge](https://gistbok-ltb.ucgis.org/current/print/concept/FC-07-026); [MAUP simulation study, PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12714233/)
- Gaussian-process density surfaces are proposed as an alternative to orthomosaic weed maps (preprint, Lincoln L-CAS). — [arXiv 2503.07210](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07210v1)
- Vendor description (not research): buffers around detections "account for GPS drift, wind-induced chemical drift, and mechanical latency" and AI outputs are rasterised "into grids that align with the physical dimensions of the sprayer equipment". — [Saiwa blog](https://saiwa.ai/blog/integrating-ai-weed-maps-with-smart-sprayer-systems/)

### Inferences
- The literature's implicit aggregation step is the grid itself: pixels are summed to a cell that equals the sprayer unit, and the threshold is applied there. Moving-window cover at a 1-3 m window (with the window stepped at 0.5 m if you want to keep the 0.5 m drawing grid) is consistent with this and with the MAUP advice to test several scales.
- Because the "extreme values average out" effect is documented, a single 20 cm plant (15 % of a 0.25 m2 cell) falls to about 1 % at 1 x 1 m, to about 0.3 % at 3 x 3 m; a true patch keeps its cover as the window grows. That is the behaviour the operator's eye already applies.
- The Salinas 2026 result cuts the other way for very small units: razor-precise spot treatment is the stronger resistance selector, so a buffered patch unit is defensible on resistance grounds too.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed paper using kernel density estimation on UAV weed detections was found in two searches; the technique exists in other hotspot mapping but is unreferenced for weed spray maps.
- No weed-mapping paper states a minimum patch area below which a detection is ignored; the closest is Paice's 1 plant/m2 density rule and Huang's threshold 0.

## Key question 3: Published economic thresholds and competitive indices, and how cover or density maps to yield loss and priority

### Takeaway
The standard model is Cousens' rectangular hyperbola with two competitiveness parameters (I = yield loss per weed at low density, A = maximum loss), from which single-year economic thresholds of about 2 plants/m2 (Galium aparine) to about 40 plants/m2 (Bromus sterilis) or 9-10 % absolute cover (wheat) are derived; however, the long-term literature (Wallinga & van Oijen 1997; Norsworthy 2012; Hamouz 2014) shows thresholds set by single-year yield loss let populations build, so priority rules must add seed return.

### Cited findings
- Cousens 1985: "a rectangular hyperbola with two agronomically meaningful parameters which together can be used as indices of competitiveness"; I = "initial slope, representing the damage per weed at low densities", A = "asymptotic maximum yield loss at high densities". Economic threshold = "the weed density at which the cost of herbicide application would just equal the financial benefit which results in that year"; because the curve is hyperbolic "a competition threshold does not exist". — [Cousens 1985 model record](https://findanexpert.unimelb.edu.au/scholarlywork/1012029-a-simple-model-relating-yield-loss-to-weed-density); [BCPC Weeds 1985 Session 7A](https://bcpc.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/BCPC-Weeds-Conference-1985-Session-7A.pdf)
- Reciprocal of I interpreted as the weed density that halves crop yield (Korean rice study). — [KoreaScience](https://koreascience.kr/article/JAKO201110348673409.do)
- Species thresholds, winter wheat (study reported as Italian in the snippet; hosted by Chilean J. Agric. Res.): Alopecurus myosuroides and Lolium multiflorum "between 25 and 35 plants m-2", Bromus sterilis "just under 40 plants m-2", Galium aparine "as low as 2 plants m-2", Vicia sativa 5-10; without nitrogen "the competitiveness of B. sterilis and G. aparine was negligible". — [CJAR 76(4) 2016](https://oes.chileanjar.cl/files/V76i4Y2016id4485.pdf)
- Turkey, winter wheat: Alopecurus myosuroides economic threshold "between 23 and 39 plants/m2" depending on herbicide cost. — [Turkish J Agric For 29(3) 2005](https://aj.tubitak.gov.tr/agriculture/issues/tar-05-29-3/tar-29-3-3-0403-10.pdf)
- Iran, wheat, combined load: economic damage threshold "5.23 TCL ... equivalent to 5.23 plants of Avena ludoviciana or 12.20 plants of Convolvulus spp. or 19.65 plants of Chenopodium album per m2". — [Univ. Tehran J](https://jijas.ut.ac.ir/article_17707.html?lang=en)
- Iran, maize vs Amaranthus: higher corn density raised the threshold "from 1.5 to 2.8 plant.m-2". — [AREEO](https://jaenph.areeo.ac.ir/article_123373.html?lang=en)
- Germany, 122 trials: ETs 9.2-9.8 % absolute cover (wheat), 4.5-8.9 % (barley). — [Keller et al. 2014](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10343-014-0335-8)
- Threshold level vs long-term control: Wallinga & van Oijen 1997, "Level of threshold weed density does not affect the long-term frequency of weed control" (Crop Protection 16:273-278): threshold "is a useful tool for cost-effective application of control on a single-year base, its role over the longer term is more complicated". — [record](https://acnpsearch.tweb-dev.unibo.it/singlejournalindex/5265285)
- Cousens, Wallinga & Shaw 2004 (Oikos): weeds in modern systems "may be managed close to critical population dynamic thresholds, below which their rates of increase will be negative". — [via Weed Science search listing](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/weed-science/article/modeling-the-effect-of-farmers-decisions-on-the-population-dynamics-of-winter-wild-oat-in-an-agricultural-landscape/6CCBB17F0B694CC798067E2A6EEE958E)
- Decision threshold as the main lever (San Martín et al. 2016, Weed Technology 30(4), maize, central Spain): raising the threshold from 6 to 12 plants m-2 gave "additional herbicide savings of up to 50%" for Sorghum halepense (patch spraying alone up to 66 %), up to 28 % for Xanthium strumarium, and only 10-20 % savings for Abutilon theophrasti; ground sensors beat aerial. — [Cambridge Weed Technology](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/weed-technology/article/weed-decision-threshold-as-a-key-factor-for-herbicide-reductions-in-sitespecific-weed-management/B89C3B35172948FADCB9CB6B152D3D33)
- Population consequence of SSWM (Hamouz et al. 2014, Czech winter crops): savings 6.3-100 % (Galium aparine), 0-84.4 % (other dicots), 31.3-90.6 % (annual monocots), but SSWM "led to significantly increased density of G. aparine and Tripleurospermum inodorum in the final experimental year" vs blanket. — [Plant Soil Environ 60(11)](https://pse.agriculturejournals.cz/artkey/pse-201411-0007_impact-of-site-specific-weed-management-in-winter-crops-on-weed-populations.php)
- Hyperbolic model fits with crop density: wild oat in wheat, parameter I "significantly lower in high-density wheat stands" (preprint). — [arXiv 2512.13718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.13718v1)
- Turkish olive guide economic framing: weeds "ağaçların suyuna ve besin maddelerine ortak olarak verimin düşmesine neden olurlar", host diseases/pests, hinder harvest and raise labour cost; Sorghum halepense singled out as "çok önemli" for deep roots, height and as host of virus-vector sucking insects (p. 56). — [TAGEM olive guide](https://www.tarimorman.gov.tr/TAGEM/Belgeler/Entegre/Zeytin%20Entegre%20M%C3%BCcadele%20Teknik%20Talimat%C4%B1.pdf)

### Inferences
- Density thresholds spread over a 20x range by species (2 plants/m2 for cleavers vs 40 for barren brome), so a cover-only map ranks a cleavers patch far too low and a brome patch too high; species or at least a "competitive broadleaf / grass / perennial" class should weight the priority.
- Cover thresholds in the 5-15 % band are all field-mean values from plot trials; they are the right order of magnitude for a patch unit of 1-9 m2, not for 0.25 m2.
- The San Martín and Huang results together show the threshold choice moves treated area more than the mapping method does, which supports making the threshold (and the unit it applies to) explicit and operator-visible.

### Gaps
- No published I/A parameter table per species was retrieved; only structure and a few derived thresholds.
- No economic threshold for weeds in orchards (apricot, olive, citrus) expressed as yield loss was found; the olive guide's 10-15 % is a practice rule without a yield-loss derivation.

## Key question 4: What makes a single weed worth treating on its own?

### Takeaway
A lone plant earns treatment through seed output and persistence (tens of thousands of seeds, seedbank half-lives of years to decades), herbicide-resistance risk (Norsworthy's BMPs: stop seed production, do not rely on single-year thresholds), perennial spread (rhizomatous Sorghum halepense in Turkish orchards), and size/stage (label efficacy falls above about 15 cm, and seed set after flowering). Dispersal is short (most seed within 2 m), so an isolated plant is a future patch at that spot.

### Cited findings
- Seeds per plant (Ohio State Extension compilation): redroot pigweed 13,000-35,000; giant ragweed 10,300; common ragweed 3,500; velvetleaf 2,000-9,000; common lambsquarters 30,000-176,000; giant foxtail 500-2,500; Canada thistle 1,500-5,300; hairy galinsoga up to 7,500. Seedbank: lambsquarters 50 % depletion in 12 years, 99 % in 78 years; pigweed 99 % in about 20 years; velvetleaf 50 % in 2 years, about 16 years for 99 %; giant foxtail 30 years for 99 %. — [OSU BYGL](https://bygl.osu.edu/node/2527)
- Averages from a 1954 North Dakota survey: lambsquarters 72,450 seeds/plant, redroot pigweed 117,400. — [UC ANR](https://ucanr.edu/node/102347)
- Kochia illustrative figure 50,000 seeds/plant. — [NDSU Carrington](https://www.ag.ndsu.edu/greenhouse/carringtonrec/center-points/2014/weed-watch-in-central-nd)
- Upper bounds: Chenopodium album "up to 600,000 seeds per plant" (agroatlas); Amaranthus retroflexus "Up to 100,000 seeds" (pestinfo wiki). — [AgroAtlas](https://agroatlas.ru/en/content/weeds/Chenopodium_album); [Pestinfo](https://wiki.pestinfo.org/wiki/Amaranthus_retroflexus_(weed))
- Viable fraction is lower than totals: 79 % green foxtail, 48 % common lambsquarters, 35 % redroot pigweed (Minnesota study as cited by OSU). — [OSU VegNet](https://u.osu.edu/vegnetnews/?p=3930)
- Timing of kill vs seed: giant foxtail cut "while flower heads were still emerging produced fewer than 10 viable seeds", fully emerged heads averaged 230 and 1,200 viable seeds/plant (2012, 2013); jimsonweed and velvetleaf "did not produce viable seed when terminated at the time of flowering". — [MSU Hill et al. WSSA 2015](https://www.canr.msu.edu/weeds/research/presented-research/HillEtAlWSSA2015.pdf)
- Dispersal: "the vast majority of seeds remain close to the source (<2 m), while a small minority can travel much farther". — [BCPC Weeds 1995 Vol III](https://www.bcpc.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/BCPC-Weeds-1995-Vol-III-Session-8B-and-9.pdf)
- Patch persistence: blackgrass seedheads monitored 10 years on a 173 ha farm; "none found on at least 60% of the surveyed positions"; significant year-to-year spatial correlation in continuous cereals; the weed persisted at low density despite herbicides and grass breaks. — [Wilson & Brain 1991, Rothamsted](https://repository.rothamsted.ac.uk/item/8w83z/long-term-stability-of-distribution-of-alopecurus-myosuroides-huds-within-cereal-fields); patchy populations "spread quickly over the entire field when no herbicide was used" and dense patches "occasionally persisted for longer than 10 yr" (González-Andújar et al. 1999 model). — [Purdue copy](https://www.stat.purdue.edu/~zhanghao/ASS/Reference%20Papers/Spatial%20and%20temporal%20dynamics%20of%20seeding%20population.pdf)
- Norsworthy et al. 2012 (Weed Science 60 SP1:31-62) BMPs as summarised by USDA APHIS: understand weed biology; diversified approach "aimed at stopping seed production and cutting the seedbank"; plant into weed-free fields and keep them weed-free; weed-free crop seed; scout routinely; multiple modes of action; "applying labeled rates at the recommended weed sizes"; crop competitiveness; mechanical/biological tools; prevent seed movement between and within fields. The paper argues for moving away from single-year economic thresholds. — [USDA APHIS BMP summary](https://WWW.APHIS.USDA.GOV/sites/default/files/aphis_bmp_recs_hr_crops.pdf); [BioOne record](https://bioone.org/journals/weed-science/volume-60/issue-sp1/WS-D-11-00155.1/Reducing-the-Risks-of-Herbicide-Resistance--Best-Management-Practices/10.1614/WS-D-11-00155.1.short)
- Precision and resistance: more accurate spot spraying "imposed stronger selection for herbicide resistance" (simulation). — [Salinas et al. 2026 record](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/do-spatially-targeted-weed-management-strategies-increase-risk-of-herbicide-resistance-evolution/)
- Size at treatment (glyphosate example, UNL): 22-32 oz "control most annual grasses 3-8 inches tall and annual broadleaves under 6 inches tall. Taller plants need the full rate", some species need 40-60 oz. — [UNL CropWatch](https://cropwatch.unl.edu/critical-period-weed-control-corn)
- Turkish olive guide: mowing "yabancı otlar çiçeklenmeden önce" at intervals so that seed set is prevented; perennial Sorghum halepense reproduces by seed and rhizome; prevent import of seed and fragments on machinery and irrigation canals in new orchards (pp. 56-57). — [TAGEM olive guide](https://www.tarimorman.gov.tr/TAGEM/Belgeler/Entegre/Zeytin%20Entegre%20M%C3%BCcadele%20Teknik%20Talimat%C4%B1.pdf)

### Inferences
- A single lambsquarters or pigweed left to seed puts tens of thousands of seeds into a 2 m radius with a seedbank half-life of 2-12 years, so "leave it" is only correct if it will be killed by something else (crop canopy, cultivation, mowing) before flowering.
- A single-plant rule that an agronomist would accept: treat it if it is (a) a perennial with rhizomes, (b) a listed high-seed or resistance-prone species, or (c) past the size the label controls and still before flowering; otherwise it is a "watch" mark, not a "treat here" mark.

### Gaps
- Norsworthy 2012 items 11 and 12 of the 12 BMPs were cut off in the only reachable summary; the Cambridge article page returned HTTP 500.
- No peer-reviewed seed-production table per species was retrieved; all per-plant numbers come from extension compilations, each citing older surveys.

## Key question 5: In orchards, what is treated and what is left (tree strip, alley cover, drip-line, Turkish guides)?

### Takeaway
Orchard practice treats the tree strip and tolerates or sows the alley: a weed-free band of about 1-1.2 m from the trunk for young trees (0.8-1.5 m found adequate for 1-3-year peach; 3.0-3.6 m is the conventional width) and 1-2 m from mature trees, while Mediterranean olive research keeps a 0.5 m herbicide strip each side of the tree line and a mown cover crop in the lane for erosion control. The Turkish olive IPM guide treats the orchard against a 10-15 % cover (or 10-15 plants/m2) orchard-mean threshold, prefers tillage or mowing-before-flowering, recommends cover crops between rows and drip irrigation, and lists only glyphosate salts as herbicides, with non-selective products barred from orchards whose trunks are not yet woody.

### Cited findings
- Young peach (North Carolina): "The common orchard floor management system in the southeastern U.S. is a 3.0 to 3.6 m wide vegetation-free strip"; with irrigation a strip of about 1.16 m matched the yield of the 3.6 m strip; tree-size results suggested "narrower strips were adequate for trees aged one to three years (0.8 to 1.5 m)". — [NCSU repository](https://repository.lib.ncsu.edu/items/338fcd5c-7376-42f6-ad8d-a5e746003215)
- Grower guidance: "at least three feet out from each side of the tree in young orchards, and at least four feet per side in mature orchards". — [Good Fruit Grower](https://goodfruit.com/careful-weed-control-is-critical-in-new-orchards)
- Oklahoma extension: strip "3 to 8 feet wide on each side of the tree" kept weed free; trunk protectors on young trees. — [OSU CR-6242](https://extension.okstate.edu/fact-sheets/weed-control-in-pecans-apples-and-peaches.html)
- Australia (HIN): "A weed free zone of 1-2 m from trees is ideal"; "young trees are especially vulnerable to weed competition". — [HIN Weed management](https://www.hin.com.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0017/18017/Weed-management.pdf)
- California (Vossen): "no weed competition be allowed within three feet of the tree trunks - ever"; managers often remove winter weeds too late. — [UC ANR](https://class.ucanr.edu/sites/default/files/2023-08/386913.pdf)
- Washington State: weeds compete "particularly so for young trees"; weed strip is "the three to eight feet wide band under the trees"; weeds plug sprinklers but "this is not a problem with drip irrigation". — [WSU Tree Fruit](https://treefruit.wsu.edu/web-article/weed-management/)
- Pecan vegetation-free circle trial in bermudagrass: "no effect 1st & 2nd year", "in the 5th year 6 ft or greater produced most yield". — [UGA pecans](https://pecans.uga.edu/content/dam/caes-subsite/pecans/docs/presentations/2016-beginners-class/orchard-establishment-wells.pdf)
- Olive, Madrid (IMIDRA, via WOCAT): a bare strip "about 0.5 m wide on each side of the tree line, treated with herbicide"; barley, sainfoin or Brachypodium distachyon sown in the alleys; "All cover crops are cut in spring, before they start to compete for water and nutrients with the crop, and mowed residues are left"; Brachypodium distachyon judged the best option (search snippet; WOCAT page is login-gated). — [WOCAT technology 1174](https://qcat.wocat.net/en/wocat/technologies/view/permalink/1174/)
- Olive, Seville (Gómez et al. 2014, CSIC/UCO/Syngenta, 6 ha irrigated table-olive orchard, 11 % slope): heterogeneous cover crop sown "in the lane" gave "a significant reduction in soil losses" vs tillage and improved plant biodiversity vs ryegrass cover; limitations "high sensibility to water erosion during the initial sowing", seed cost, and "the need to evaluate the risk of competition for soil water with the olives". — [ELS2014-34-1](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/ELS2014/ELS2014-34-1.pdf)
- Olive, Córdoba 2000-2006 runoff plots: less runoff with cover crops than bare fallow or conventional tillage. — [Conservation Evidence 4237](https://www.conservationevidence.com/individual-study/4237)
- Turkish olive IPM guide (TAGEM 2022), weed control section (pp. 56-57): priorities are cultural (clean machinery, canal-bank weeds, well-rotted manure, "damla sulamanın kullanımı yabancı ot çıkışını büyük oranda azaltmaktadır"), mechanical ("Sürüm: Bahçelerde genellikle en fazla tavsiye edilen mekanik mücadele yöntemidir", shallow so as not to damage roots; on slopes "biçme", cut "çiçeklenmeden önce"), physical (mulch, mainly nurseries; cover crops sown "sıra aralarına", typically Fabaceae such as berseem, common vetch, salad burnet, or Poaceae), then chemical: pre-emergence "İlkbaharda bahçe sürülüp hazırlandıktan ... sonra toprak nemli iken", post-emergence during active growth; "Seçici olmayan ... herbisitleri uygularken ağaçların yaprak, sürgün ve meyvelerine ilaçlı karışımın gelmemesine dikkat edilmeli ve bu herbisitler gövdesi odunlaşmamış zeytin bahçelerinde kullanılmamalıdır"; apply at 8-25 °C, calm air, knapsack sprayer with flat-fan nozzle (p. 58). Ek-4 lists only glyphosate (ammonium salt 748 g/l WP 200 g/da; potassium salt 441 g/l SL 300 ml/da; acid 360 g/l SL 500 ml/da) for annual and perennial grass and broadleaf weeds. Tillage, irrigation and fertiliser are directed to the "taç izdüşümü" (canopy drip-line projection). — [TAGEM olive guide](https://www.tarimorman.gov.tr/TAGEM/Belgeler/Entegre/Zeytin%20Entegre%20M%C3%BCcadele%20Teknik%20Talimat%C4%B1.pdf)
- Turkish IPM principle (all crop guides): chemical control "tavsiye edilmemektedir" where alternatives work; the aim is to hold weeds "Ekonomik Zarar Eşiğinin altında", not to eliminate them. — [Kilis İl Müdürlüğü brochure](https://kilis.tarim.gov.tr/Belgeler/Broşür%20ve%20Afişler/Entegre%20Mücadele.pdf)
- Apricot, Malatya (Kale district, 6 orchards, 2020-2022): 106 weed species in 30 families; dominant Ranunculus repens, R. bulbosus, Poa bulbosa, Cynodon dactylon; Malatya has about 8 million apricot trees and 46.2 % of national production. No apricot-specific IPM technical instruction was found. — [İnönü/Özal University thesis record](https://acikerisim.ozal.edu.tr/items/b535f457-2fd3-439c-a81a-ac8e6252416b)
- Citrus, Turkey: Uygur et al., "Turunçgillerde Yabancı Otlar ve Entegre Mücadelesi", pp. 121-157 in Türkiye Turunçgil Bahçelerinde Entegre Mücadele (TÜBİTAK, 2001); Özkan 1997 "Turunçgil Bahçelerinde Entegre Mücadele Teknik Talimatları" (library record only); Akdeniz University study of cover plants and mulch for integrated weed control in Çukurova citrus (record only). — [Çukurova record](https://avesis.cu.edu.tr/activitycitation/index/1/b8fc1b3f-8163-417d-8cca-efb49da96265); [Library record](https://kutuphane.tarimorman.gov.tr/vufind/Record/1217356/Similar); [Akdeniz record](https://avesis.akdeniz.edu.tr/yayin/a3ea9f20-ebab-40a0-82e7-5c3c7dc45700/cukurova-bolgesi-turuncgil-bahcelerinde-ortucu-bitki-ve-malc-uygulamalarinin-entegre-yabanci-ot-kontrolu-acisindan-degerlendirilmesi)

### Inferences
- For a young apricot orchard the actionable zone is a band of roughly 1-1.5 m from each trunk (the 0.8-1.5 m peach finding, the 1-2 m HIN rule, the 0.9 m "three feet" rule); alley cover outside that band should be reported as cover, not as "treat here", unless it is a listed perennial (Sorghum halepense, Cynodon dactylon) or the operator has declared the alley bare-floor.
- Because the Turkish guide bars non-selective herbicides from orchards with unlignified trunks, a young-orchard "treat here" mark should default to mechanical (hoe/mow) wording, consistent with the product rule of never naming a rate.

### Gaps
- No Turkish apricot or citrus IPM technical instruction PDF was reachable; the olive guide is the only primary Turkish ministry text read in full.
- No orchard study quantifies yield loss from weeds specifically at drip emitters; sources only note drip reduces weed emergence and emitter blockage is not an issue.

## Key question 6: What ranking scheme would an agronomist recognise as credible for "treat first / treat next / leave"? (incl. critical period and emergence timing)

### Takeaway
No single published scoring formula was found; what the literature supports is a lexical ranking: (1) is the unit inside the crop's critical period and the weed inside the label size window, (2) is the patch above the field/orchard cover or density threshold at a sprayer-scale unit, (3) species class (perennial/rhizomatous and high-seed or resistance-prone first), (4) proximity to the crop (tree strip vs alley; within-row vs inter-row), (5) patch persistence/history. Singles are "watch" unless rule 3 or seed-set timing promotes them.

### Cited findings
- Critical period, corn (Nebraska, 5 % loss): V1-V11 (8-45 days after emergence) at 0 N; V3-V10 at 55 lb N; V4-V9 at 110; V6-V9 (20-39 DAE) at 210 lb N; "an average 2% in yield per every leaf stage of delay"; weeds taller than corn: start 4-5 days before CPWC; weeds emerging 5-8 days after crop: start 5-10 days after CPWC begins; control recommended up to canopy closure (about 11 leaves). — [UNL CropWatch](https://cropwatch.unl.edu/critical-period-weed-control-corn)
- Critical period, soybean (Nebraska, 30-inch rows): starts at V1 "about 9 days after the crop emerges", window roughly 9-19 DAE across row widths; "about 2% for each leaf-stage of delay past the optimum weed control time, up until the beginning pod stage" (extension/press summary). Velvetleaf emerging at VC, V1, V3 cut soybean yield 47 %, 38 %, 15 %. — [Farm Progress](https://www.farmprogress.com/soybean/delayed-soybean-weed-control-costs-3-50-per-day); [UNL velvetleaf](https://cropwatch.unl.edu/soybean-row-spacing-affects-velvetleaf-competitiveness)
- Critical period, cotton (Kahramanmaraş, Turkey; Tursun et al. 2016, Phytoparasitica 44:139-149; 5 % loss): 50 cm rows 16-52 DAE (117-526 GDD 2012); 70 cm rows 15-60 DAE; 90 cm rows 11-67 DAE; narrow rows more competitive. — [İnönü repository](https://abakus.inonu.edu.tr/items/8469c3fa-1f4a-4523-ad22-fd871de7730f)
- Critical period concept paper: Knezevic et al. 2002, Weed Science 50:773-786, defines CPWC as the period "in which weeds must be controlled to avoid unacceptable yield loss". — [Cambridge](https://cambridge.org/core/journals/weed-science/article/critical-period-for-weed-control-the-concept-and-data-analysis/AB3851B73BA3BB8FC602A042802EC412)
- Threshold is the biggest lever, species matter: 6 vs 12 plants/m2 changed savings by up to 50 % for johnsongrass but velvetleaf patches gave only 10-20 % savings. — [San Martín et al. 2016](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/weed-technology/article/weed-decision-threshold-as-a-key-factor-for-herbicide-reductions-in-sitespecific-weed-management/B89C3B35172948FADCB9CB6B152D3D33)
- Unit upper bound: 6 x 6 m for long-term profitability (blackgrass simulation). — [Paice et al. 1998](https://repository.rothamsted.ac.uk/item/87y3q/a-stochastic-simulation-model-for-evaluating-the-concept-of-patch-spraying)
- Patches are stable and the usual mapping unit for repeat treatment: patchy distribution "remained generally stable over ten years". — [Wilson & Brain 1991](https://repository.rothamsted.ac.uk/item/8w83z/long-term-stability-of-distribution-of-alopecurus-myosuroides-huds-within-cereal-fields)
- Seed-set timing as a hard deadline (foxtail <10 viable seeds if cut at head emergence; velvetleaf none at flowering). — [MSU 2015](https://www.canr.msu.edu/weeds/research/presented-research/HillEtAlWSSA2015.pdf)
- Resistance BMP: labeled rates at recommended weed sizes; stop seed production; scout. — [USDA APHIS summary of Norsworthy 2012](https://WWW.APHIS.USDA.GOV/sites/default/files/aphis_bmp_recs_hr_crops.pdf)
- Orchard zoning: strip of 0.8-1.5 m adequate for 1-3-year trees; 1-2 m from trees ideal (HIN); alley cover mown in spring before water competition (IMIDRA/WOCAT). — [NCSU](https://repository.lib.ncsu.edu/items/338fcd5c-7376-42f6-ad8d-a5e746003215); [HIN](https://www.hin.com.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0017/18017/Weed-management.pdf); [WOCAT 1174](https://qcat.wocat.net/en/wocat/technologies/view/permalink/1174/)
- Orchard-level decision rule: treat when orchard mean is >= 10-15 plants/m2 or 10-15 % cover; Sorghum halepense flagged as the most important species. — [TAGEM olive guide](https://www.tarimorman.gov.tr/TAGEM/Belgeler/Entegre/Zeytin%20Entegre%20M%C3%BCcadele%20Teknik%20Talimat%C4%B1.pdf)

### Inferences (proposed ranking, each line traceable to the findings above)
- Unit: compute cover in a moving window equal to the treatment unit (orchard: the tree-strip band, about 1-1.5 m either side of the trunk line, in 1-3 m lengths; row crops: nozzle-section width across rows x 1-3 m along, never above 6 x 6 m). Keep the 0.5 m grid for drawing, not for deciding.
- Treat first: patch cover >= crop-group threshold at the unit scale, AND inside the tree strip / crop row zone, AND (crop inside its critical period OR weed approaching flowering), OR any perennial-rhizomatous patch (Sorghum halepense, Cynodon dactylon) regardless of cover.
- Treat next: patch above threshold but in the alley or after canopy closure; or below threshold but a high-seed/resistance-prone species still inside the label size window.
- Leave (watch): isolated plants below the unit threshold, in the alley, not a listed perennial, and either small enough to be shaded out or past the point where label control works; re-check before flowering.
- Score ordering, if a numeric score is wanted: species class weight > cover at unit scale x patch area > proximity (strip/row vs alley) > growth-stage urgency > persistence (seen in a previous scan at the same place). The literature supports the order, not specific weights; weights would be the product's choice and should be labelled as such.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed paper was found that publishes a multi-factor patch priority index (size x cover x species x distance); the ranking above is a synthesis.
- Wheat critical-period data were not retrieved; the Knezevic/Datta 2015 methods paper and Gerhards 2022 review were citation-only.
- The Lutman "seed return" threshold concept was not located in any reachable source.
