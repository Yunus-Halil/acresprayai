# Segmenting and measuring individual weeds from original drone frames (1-2 cm/px, DJI RGB, nadir, overlapping)

Scope: methods and numbers for (a) segmenting weeds in high-resolution RGB drone frames, classical and learned; (b) what can be measured from a segmented weed in a nadir frame and how well; (c) fusing measurements of one weed across overlapping frames; (d) datasets and licences for validation. Research only. Every number below carries its source; where a primary source could not be opened the fact is placed under Gaps rather than invented.

Note on access: the University of Bonn PDF host (ipb.uni-bonn.de) refused connections throughout this run, ScienceDirect and DOAJ returned 403, and bioRxiv rate-limited until the last attempt. Where a secondary summary is the only source, it is marked as such.

## Key question 1: At 1-2 cm/px, what weed size can be segmented reliably, and where do classical index methods stop working?

### Takeaway
The published rule of thumb is that individual weed seedlings need roughly 1-4 cm pixels and a seedling must span several pixels (a ~3 cm seedling in a ~5 cm pixel is a mixed pixel and is lost); at 1-2 cm/px a weed of ~5 cm canopy diameter is 3-5 px across and is at the edge of what any method resolves, while ~10 cm and larger is comfortably segmentable. Classical ExG/ExGR/CIVE thresholding reaches ~87-94 % pixel accuracy on well-lit green-on-soil scenes and degrades under dark shadows, crop residue and when weeds touch or sit inside the crop canopy; those are exactly the cases where learned models (or a learned crop mask plus an index for the rest) are needed.

### Cited Findings
- Peña et al. (2015) flew RGB and multispectral cameras at 40 and 100 m giving pixel sizes of 1.52-3.81 cm (RGB) and 2.16-5.41 cm (multispectral); when a weed seedling of about 3 cm is smaller than a ~5.4 cm pixel, soil and weed are mixed in the pixel and weed objects may not be created — [Peña et al. 2015, Quantifying Efficacy and Limits of UAV Technology for Weed Seedling Detection as Affected by Sensor Resolution](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/273139881_Quantifying_Efficacy_and_Limits_of_Unmanned_Aerial_Vehicle_UAV_Technology_for_Weed_Seedling_Detection_as_Affected_by_Sensor_Resolution)
- A Weed Technology review of that work states that discriminating individual weed plants needs a pixel size of about 1-4 cm (40-100 m altitude for RGB, 40-60 m for multispectral), while weed patches tolerate 5 cm or coarser pixels (roughly 100 m or higher) — [Huang et al. 2018, UAV Low-Altitude Remote Sensing for Precision Weed Management, Weed Technology](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/weed-technology/article/uav-lowaltitude-remote-sensing-for-precision-weed-management/6C81EEBF460FF0FC9E48F51E331425C5)
- López-Granados and colleagues state that pixel size for early weed detection must be 5 cm or less — [Imagery from UAV for early site-specific weed management](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/260932073_Imagery_from_unmanned_aerial_vehicles_UAV_for_early_site_specific_weed_management_ESSWM)
- A random-forest OBIA study at 30 m (0.6 cm orthomosaic pixel) and 60 m (1.2 cm) found the 60 m results inferior and concluded high spatial resolution is crucial for automatic early-stage weed mapping — [de Castro et al. 2018, Remote Sensing 10(2):285](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs10020285)
- In maize plant detection from UAV frames with Faster R-CNN, native 0.3 cm/px gave density rRMSE 0.08 and 0.6 cm/px (synthetic, downsampled) rRMSE 0.11; native 0.6 cm/px imagery scored rRMSE 0.48 "mainly because of their quality"; GAN super-resolution of native low-res reached 0.22; the authors state high-resolution images are required to detect small plants at early stages — [Velumani et al. 2021, arXiv 2105.11857](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.11857)
- PhenoBench is captured at 1 mm/px (about 21 m, PhaseOne iXM-100, 80 mm lens on a DJI M600); even at that resolution weed IoU is 64.37 (ERFNet) and 64.59 (DeepLabV3+) against crop IoU 94.30 / 94.07, because weed pixels are often assigned to crop; weed detection AP is 17.62 (Faster R-CNN), 17.05 (Mask R-CNN), 37.91 (YOLOv7) against crop AP 63.23 / 60.32 / 83.06, attributed to smaller plant sizes; instance difficulty is attributed to "barely visible small plants and large plants with heavy mutual overlap" — [Weyler et al., PhenoBench, arXiv 2306.04557v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.04557v2)
- ExG combined with Otsu thresholding reached 87.52 % accuracy for canopy cover, below ExG-ExR with a manual threshold at 94.69 % (ground-based imagery) — [Univ. of Tehran journal article 87641](https://journals.ut.ac.ir/article_87641_dd04186b1426cc39291f08aba93025c8.pdf)
- A UAV pipeline used ExG-ExR with minimum-error thresholding for green-plant segmentation at 93.5 % accuracy, then a modified U-Net to split crop from weed — [Remote Sensing, Jan 2021 (DOAJ record)](https://doaj.org/article/40849e1dd13b459d8c171355b153585e)
- ExG-based segmentation "often performs poorly when dark shadows create strong illumination differences"; an illumination-invariant transform with Otsu thresholding was proposed instead — [WUR, Shadow-resistant segmentation based on illumination-invariant image](https://research.wur.nl/en/publications/shadow-resistant-segmentation-based-on-illumination-invariant-ima/)
- The 2004 ExGExR index (ExG minus ExR, zero threshold) "performed better than the ExG index for segmenting plants from soil-residue background" — [Meyer et al. 2004 (Embrapa catalogue record)](https://www.bdpa.cnptia.embrapa.br/consulta/marc?id=9026)
- In shadowed desert UAV scenes an HSV-GLCM-SVM method reached 0.91 accuracy against ExG and CIVE baselines (baseline values not in the abstract) — [PMC12843393](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12843393/)
- In a UAV citrus orchard, chromatic aberration plus Otsu then SVM gave 85.27 % ± 9.43 % segmentation accuracy across varying brightness and weed coverage — [PMC6960911](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6960911/)
- A 2025-era weed paper notes weeds often share colour, texture and shape with crops, giving pixel-level confusion; its best setup was a learned crop mask with HSV-based weed filtering for the remainder, which "substantially reduced annotation requirements" — [AgriEngineering 8:228](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/agriengineering/agriengineering-08-00228/article_deploy/agriengineering-08-00228.pdf)
- Marker-controlled watershed with colour-index markers and edge enhancement separated leaves from a complex background at a 74.1 % success rate versus 0 % for classical watershed and 13.8 % for ordinary marker-controlled watershed — [Abdullah 2018, Improving Leaf Segmentation by Creating the Automatic Marker](https://unisep.lib.unishams.edu.my/xmlui/handle/123456789/10995?show=full)
- "The higher the marker precision, the better the segmentation results" for watershed vs graph cut on leaves, and background elimination is hard because the background often contains other leaves of the same type — [arXiv 1709.06437](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.06437)
- A Swedish method for sugar beet occluded by weeds cuts partly occluded leaves at the broadest region between them rather than relying on watershed alone — [Jordbruksverket report Fil-001173](https://fou.jordbruksverket.se/fou/dl/Fil-001173)
- Instance-segmentation of weeds in a potato field from a DJI Phantom 4 RTK at 10 m (GSD 0.27 cm/px) reached mAP50 0.920 (Mask R-CNN) and 0.902 (YOLOv8; 0.909 with 2x Real-ESRGAN super-resolution); per-class potato 0.983 vs weed 0.821; splitting weeds into "long" and "other" dropped mAP50 to 0.860; pixel-level MIoU was only 0.515; training set 130 images of 640x640 px with 659 potato and 934 weed instances; the authors name visual similarity, class imbalance, overlapping plants and fine-grained small weed parts as failure causes — [Weed Instance Segmentation from UAV ortho-mosaic Images, bioRxiv 2024.08.13.607729](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.08.13.607729v1.full-text); published version at [Smart Agricultural Technology, S2772375525001996](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772375525001996)
- In a 4 ha wheat field (Mongolia, UAV RGB, three weed species) Mask R-CNN and YOLOv8 at IoU 0.5 gave precision 0.42-0.67, recall 0.18-0.75, F1 0.28-0.69; Mask R-CNN had higher recall and better boundaries, YOLOv8 higher precision and ~11 ms/image — [preprints.org 202405.0018](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202405.0018)
- A ground-level laser-weeding YOLOv8-seg model improved mAP50 over Mask R-CNN, YOLOv5-seg and YOLOv7-seg by 10.8 %, 13.4 % and 1.8 % — [Frontiers in Sustainable Food Systems 2024, 10.3389/fsufs.2024.1433701](https://frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2024.1433701/full)
- Training-data note: PhenoBench's 2,872 labelled images (1,407 train / 772 val / 693 test, 1024x1024 crops from 11,664x8,750 px frames) are what the published ERFNet/Mask R-CNN/Mask2Former baselines above were trained on — [PhenoBench arXiv 2306.04557v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.04557v2)

### Inferences
- Scaling the Peña rule: at 1 cm/px the published seedling-resolution floor is met for weeds of roughly 3-4 cm; at 2 cm/px that floor moves to ~6-8 cm. Weeds below ~3 px across cannot be separated from mixed soil/vegetation pixels by any index; a model trained at finer GSD will also lose them (PhenoBench weed AP 17-38 at 1 mm/px shows weeds are the hard class even at ten times finer resolution).
- The classical chain that the literature supports at Swardus's GSD is: ExGR (not ExG) with Otsu or minimum-error threshold, an illumination-invariant transform or shadow mask when the frame has hard shadows, and marker-controlled watershed only with carefully made markers (distance transform or crown peaks), since plain watershed scores near zero on touching leaves.
- Index methods stop being trustworthy in four places: hard shadow (dark vegetation falls below threshold), crop-weed contact (index gives one blob; only the row model or a learned instance model separates them), senescing or drying weeds (no longer green; nothing in the literature retrieved gives numbers for this case), and green residue or algae on wet soil (not quantified in sources found). The potato study's pixel MIoU of 0.515 next to mAP50 0.92 shows that learned instance models find weeds but draw loose boundaries, which matters for area measurement.
- Mask R-CNN vs YOLO-seg: evidence is thin and mixed; one UAV study favours Mask R-CNN for recall and boundaries, the other near-identical mAP50. Neither clearly wins at this GSD; what dominates is the training set (130 images sufficed for mAP50 0.9 in one potato field, but the wheat study with three species stayed at F1 under 0.7).

### Gaps
- No source quantified ExG/ExGR/CIVE failure on senescent (yellow/brown) weeds or on crop residue at drone GSD; the ExGExR residue result is from 2004 ground imagery and only "better than ExG" is reported.
- The Chinese-cabbage YOLOv8-seg result (86.3 % mAP, maintained at 1.14-1.33 cm/px) appeared in a search summary but its URL could not be tied to a specific paper; treat as unconfirmed.
- No study compared RF-DETR-seg against Mask R-CNN or YOLO-seg on weed UAV imagery; the only RF-DETR numbers are Roboflow's own.
- No publication gives a weed-size vs recall curve at 1-2 cm/px; the Peña and Velumani results are the nearest proxies.

## Key question 2: Does SAM / SAM 2 work zero-shot on drone weed imagery, and with what prompting?

### Takeaway
On top-view field imagery, plain SAM / SAM 2 zero-shot does not reach supervised quality: on PhenoBench, Grounded-SAM scores AP50 13.8 (PQ plant 19.95) and Grounded-SAM 2 AP50 15.9 (PQ 18.3) against a supervised 37.4 (PQ 41.7); prompting matters more than the model (box prompts from a detector generally beat points, two positive plus two negative points beat more points, automatic mask generation is worst), and the one agricultural study that specifically tried it treats SAM as a mask proposer and classifies weeds as anomalies relative to crop features. SAM is useful as a boundary refiner given a good prompt (an index blob or a detector box), not as a weed finder.

### Cited Findings
- On PhenoBench (UAV sugar beet), zero-shot Grounded-SAM scored Prec50 65.21 / Rec50 14.89 / AP50 13.80 / PQ plant 19.95; Grounded-SAM 2 scored 18.80 / 30.08 / 15.89 / 18.27; Leaf-Only-SAM scored AP50 4.99; the supervised HAPT baseline scored AP50 37.40 / PQ plant 41.66; the heuristic ZeroPlantSeg reached AP50 25.32 / PQ 32.21 — [ZeroPlantSeg, arXiv 2509.09116](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09116v1)
- On the UAV cauliflower set GrowliFlower (no training), Grounded-SAM reached AP50 54.92 / PQ plant 48.90 while the PhenoBench-trained supervised model collapsed to AP50 14.60; on the robot sugar beet set SB20, Grounded-SAM AP50 18.22 / PQ 25.47 and Grounded-SAM 2 22.47 / 24.79 — [ZeroPlantSeg, arXiv 2509.09116](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09116v1)
- The same paper states that obtaining masks for instances small relative to image size "is challenging, which is likely to occur in images captured from UAVs", handled with a sliding-window crop plus upsampling; dense planting and leaf overlap were the hardest condition; the method is rosette-plant-specific and does not address weeds — [ZeroPlantSeg, arXiv 2509.09116](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09116v1)
- Chong et al. (Bonn, IROS 2025) use SAM (ViT-L checkpoint) and BioCLIP to build a bag of crop features and infer weeds as anomalies (low similarity to crop hyperspheres), evaluated on PhenoBench, SB20, CropAndWeed-SugarBeet and CropAndWeed-Maize without labels; code is MIT-licensed — [GitHub PRBonn/WeedsAreWeird](https://github.com/PRBonn/WeedsAreWeird); paper PDF at [ipb.uni-bonn.de (not reachable this run)](https://www.ipb.uni-bonn.de/pdfs/chong2025iros.pdf); venue listed as IROS 2025 by Bonn and CVPRW 2025 by [Lamarr Institute](https://lamarr-institute.org/publication/zero-shot-semantic-segmentation-for-robots-in-agriculture/)
- A search-engine summary of the Chong PDF reports its SAM-based vegetation segmentation had the highest vegetation IoU among compared methods and beat per-pixel thresholding baselines on most datasets, but the per-dataset weed IoU table was unreadable in the snippet (unverified) — [chong2025iros.pdf](https://www.ipb.uni-bonn.de/pdfs/chong2025iros.pdf)
- For rice seedlings in UAV imagery, point prompts gave the most accurate maps, boxes next, automatic mask generation last — [ISPRS Annals X-G-2025, 713](https://isprs-annals.copernicus.org/articles/X-G-2025/713/2025/isprs-annals-X-G-2025-713-2025.pdf)
- For plant instance segmentation with Grounding DINO + SAM, the optimal point configuration was two positive and two negative points; performance degraded beyond that — [Frontiers in Plant Science 2025, 10.3389/fpls.2025.1536226](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/plant-science/articles/10.3389/fpls.2025.1536226/full)
- An Agricultural SAM Adapter paper reports SAM "performs notably better with box prompts than with point prompts" and that single-prompt performance "highly varies depending on the dataset and the task" (CWFID-based evaluation) — [PMC10534855](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10534855/)
- Grounding DINO (Swin-T) + SAM2-Large reached mIoU 0.694 ± 0.175 on fescue grass and 0.905 ± 0.114 on red-leaf disease in wild blueberry fields, consistently beating YOLO-World as the prompter — [PMC12694221](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12694221/)
- A strawberry paper notes SAM2 "relies heavily on manual expert prompts to achieve class-specific segmentation", limiting automation — [PMC12736712](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12736712/)
- Using Pl@ntNet/DINOv2 features to make coarse plant masks that prompt SAM2 reached Jaccard up to 0.754 on an apple-tree dataset, evaluated across four datasets from sparse top-view sugar beet (PhenoBench) to dense apple orchard — [arXiv 2510.12579](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.12579v1)
- Carraro, Sozzi and Marinello evaluated SAM zero-shot for precision-agriculture semantic segmentation (Smart Agricultural Technology vol. 6, 100367, Dec 2023); the numbers could not be retrieved (403) — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/31c0812fab76401faa352f8f6e01ff6b)
- A generative-pipeline paper describes SAM as a zero-shot proposer feeding weed-control training data rather than a weed classifier in itself — [arXiv 2411.00548](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.00548)

### Inferences
- For Swardus the usable pattern is: index mask (ExGR + Otsu) or row-model residual gives candidate blobs; blob centroid becomes a positive point and nearby crop-row pixels become negative points, or the blob's bounding box becomes a box prompt; SAM / SAM 2 returns a tighter instance mask than the index blob. The Bonn "weeds are anomalies" design is the closest published analogue and its code is MIT.
- Do not rely on SAM automatic mask generation over whole frames: it is ranked worst in every agricultural comparison found and misses small objects at UAV scale without tiling.
- SAM 2 was not better than SAM 1 on still frames in the one head-to-head found (PhenoBench PQ 18.3 vs 19.95); its advantage is video tracking, which is not relevant to independent nadir frames.

### Gaps
- No agricultural study reports zero-shot SAM IoU specifically for weeds (as opposed to crops or plants) on UAV frames at 1-2 cm/px; the Chong paper is the nearest and its table was unreadable.
- Carraro et al. 2023 numbers not retrieved.
- No evaluation of SAM prompting from an ExG/ExGR mask was found; the index-to-prompt step would be a Swardus-side experiment.

## Key question 3: Which measurements from a nadir RGB frame are trustworthy and which are not?

### Takeaway
Projected canopy area (and the diameter derived from it) is the one quantity a single nadir frame measures directly, given the frame's scale; the literature supports it as a proxy for leaf area and, through a linear relation, for biomass in early growth, with the explicit caveat that leaf overlap makes the relation go non-linear as plants grow. Height and biomass are not measurable from one frame; biomass needs a species-and-stage-specific calibration, and no published weed allometry transferable to Swardus's species list was found.

### Cited Findings
- For purple nutsedge, a linear relation between measured leaf-cover area and plant biomass held regardless of developmental stage; the method converts images to an illumination-invariant representation and to an object-space frame so measurement is in metric units from any camera position — [Lati et al., Weed Science 59(2), Robust Methods for Measurement of Leaf-Cover Area and Biomass from Image Data](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/weed-science/article/abs/robust-methods-for-measurement-of-leafcover-area-and-biomass-from-image-data/C8686BF3ADE502A3F9B3DA41224D32C5)
- Leaf cover, the vertical projection of the canopy on the ground, "can be used to approximate leaf area at early stages of plant development" — [Weed Technology, Validation of an operator-assisted module to measure weed and crop leaf cover](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/weed-technology/article/validation-of-an-operatorassisted-module-to-measure-weed-and-crop-leaf-cover-by-digital-image-analysis/DFEAADAB76B2B2C0FD4F6BBDBFA8BF74)
- For lettuce seedlings, correlations between direct biomass and camera-derived leaf-area estimates were r = 0.76-0.94 in field conditions — [ASHS 2010 abstract 4285](https://ashs.confex.com/ashs/2010/webprogramarchives/Paper4285.html)
- For Old World bluestem, predicted LAI and biomass "increased exponentially with ground cover, likely owing to overlapping leaf area in older canopies" — [Agronomy Journal, 10.2134/agronj2018.08.0502](https://acsess.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2134/agronj2018.08.0502)
- A strong linear canopy-area to above-ground-biomass relation (r² = 0.96) was observed for oneseed juniper, and crown area was the best single AGB predictor across species in an African savanna shrub study — [PMC7054341](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7054341/); [ScienceDirect S2666719323000092](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666719323000092)
- In grassland, a structure-from-motion volumetric model gave R² 0.93 (fresh) and 0.94 (dry biomass) while a vegetation-index regression gave R² 0.80 and 0.66 — [PLOS ONE 10.1371/journal.pone.0245784](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0245784)
- Strawberry dry biomass was modelled by multiple linear regression on canopy size metrics delineated from high-resolution imagery, with weed effects handled in image processing — [Remote Sensing 12:3632](https://doi.org/10.3390/RS12213632)
- A hydroponic-lettuce study counts plant pixels per object for projected canopy area from top-view RGB-D and constrains the biomass model with an allometric canopy-area-to-biomass scaling loss — [ScienceDirect S0168169925004053](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0168169925004053)
- Dry biomass is consistently harder to predict than fresh: oats AGB-fresh R² ~0.80 vs AGB-dry ~0.70 — [Sensors 22:601](https://dx.doi.org/10.3390/s22020601); semi-arid grassland dry biomass R² 0.69 (XGBoost) — [MDPI 2813-3463/4/4/48](https://www.mdpi.com/2813-3463/4/4/48)
- Herbage-mass estimation used per-image soil %, grass %, clover % and weed % as regression features, i.e. cover fractions by class rather than per-plant allometry — [arXiv 2110.13719](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.13719)
- Individual plant height from UAV RGB vs ground truth in an intercropped field: R² 0.86 (cabbage, RMSE 6.75 cm), 0.94 (pumpkin, 6.99 cm), 0.36 (barley, 14.16 cm), 0.49 (wheat, 22.04 cm) — [Agriculture 12(1):102](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-0472/12/1/102)
- Individual Chinese-cabbage plant weight from multi-temporal UAV features: R² 0.86, RMSE 436 g/plant — [Scientific Reports, Nov 2023 (DOAJ record)](https://doaj.org/article/3513c6ffb394421d85cfd23b42f73ba3)
- Pistachio canopy volume from planar canopy area plus ground shadows reached R² > 0.8 (pixel-based) and > 0.9 (OBIA) against ground-truth tree dimensions — [ISPRS Archives XL-1/W4, 299 (2015)](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XL-1-W4/299/2015/)
- In cotton, RGB-derived plant height correlated with manual measurements at r² 0.89 and 0.94 on two dates, while RGB vs NDVI canopy-cover agreement over the season was low (r² 0.37) — [Beltwide Cotton 2018 paper 18440](https://www.cotton.org/beltwide/proceedings/2005-2022/data/conferences/2018/papers/18440.pdf)
- Learned instance masks can be loose: in the potato UAV study mAP50 was 0.92 but pixel MIoU only 0.515 and mean Dice 0.53, which bounds the area accuracy obtainable from those masks — [bioRxiv 2024.08.13.607729](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.08.13.607729v1.full-text)

### Inferences
- Trustworthy from one nadir frame at known scale: pixel-counted projected canopy area, equivalent-circle diameter, major/minor axis, and compactness. Their error is dominated by mask quality (an index mask under-covers shadowed leaves; a loose learned mask over-covers) and by scale error (the frame's GSD from flight height, which varies with terrain and with the gimbal's true tilt).
- Reasonable as a ranked proxy, not a number: leaf area (linear with cover only while the weed is small and leaves do not overlap) and relative biomass within one species and stage (every source finds a monotone relation but the slope is species- and stage-specific, and the bluestem result shows it goes exponential once leaves overlap).
- Not measurable from one nadir frame: height (no vertical information), absolute biomass (needs calibration the product does not have), growth stage beyond coarse size classes. This matches the product's "decision support, not prescriptions" line: report area and size class, never a weight.
- Published allometries exist for shrubs, trees, grasses and crops; none found for the broadleaf arable weeds of interest. Any area-to-biomass figure Swardus showed would be its own calibration, which the notes should say plainly.

### Gaps
- No published area-to-biomass regression with R² for Chenopodium, Amaranthus or other common arable weeds from photographs; the nutsedge paper gives "linear" without the R² in the abstract and the full text was not retrievable.
- No study validating individual-weed canopy area from UAV RGB against a leaf-area meter or hand measurement was found; all per-plant validation found was for crops (cabbage height, cabbage weight, lettuce seedling leaf area).
- No source quantified scale error from gimbal tilt or terrain on per-plant area in a single frame.

## Key question 4: How is canopy height estimated from overlapping drone frames and what accuracy is reported for small plants?

### Takeaway
Height comes from a SfM dense point cloud or DSM minus a ground DTM (CHM), not from single frames; reported RMSE is ~3.5 cm at best for small plots flown low with ground control, 6-8 cm for individual vegetable plants, and 12-20 cm at higher altitude or for canopy tops; a 5-10 cm weed is therefore inside the error band. Oblique (45-60°) imagery, low altitude and sharp frames improve it; shadow length is a published alternative only for trees and is biased by slope.

### Cited Findings
- SfM plant heights agreed with manual measurements at RMSE 3.5 cm with slight underestimation, attributed to coarser UAV resolution and poorer canopy penetration than LiDAR — [PMC5711830](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5711830/)
- The companion study reports LiDAR-to-manual RMSE 3.47 cm and SfM DTM errors mostly 2.6-6.8 cm — [Frontiers in Plant Science 2017, 10.3389/fpls.2017.02002](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/plant-science/articles/10.3389/fpls.2017.02002/full)
- Faba bean plot height RMSE 7.52 cm (3D RGB) and 8.05 cm (2D RGB) — [Plant Methods 2022, 10.1186/s13007-022-00861-7](https://plantmethods.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13007-022-00861-7)
- Fixed-wing at 120 m over sorghum: RMSE generally under 0.20 m; multi-level ground-control calibration cut RMSE by about 20 % — [Sensors 18:4092](https://doi.org/10.3390/s18124092)
- Multitemporal maize/sorghum canopy-level errors ~0.12-0.16 m against terrestrial laser scanning — [ScienceDirect S0303243417301800](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0303243417301800)
- Individual cabbage plant height R² 0.86 / RMSE 6.75 cm, pumpkin 0.94 / 6.99 cm, barley 0.36 / 14.16 cm, wheat 0.49 / 22.04 cm — [Agriculture 12(1):102](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-0472/12/1/102)
- Cabbage time-series: individual plant height from an SfM DSM minus soil height — [ScienceDirect S2772375524000480](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772375524000480) (RMSE not in the abstract; full text 403)
- Oblique 45° imagery gave higher height accuracy than nadir 90°, especially late season, and 40 m beat 80 m on every date — [Drones 8:746](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/drones/drones-08-00746/article_deploy/drones-08-00746.pdf)
- Rice SfM point clouds from −60° camera sets had smaller 3D coordinate errors than nadir sets — [Breeding Research (J-STAGE) vol 24 no 1](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/browse/jsbbr/24/1/_contents/-char/en)
- In sorghum, image blur "appeared to have a significant impact on the accuracy of plant height estimation" — [Sensors, Nov 2018 (DOAJ record)](https://doaj.org/article/10c10775975d4a5dbfb4a672e37dfa5f)
- Tree height from shadow length on a UAV orthomosaic (pine plantation): RRMSE 5.6 % with slope correction vs 8.2 % without; CHM approach 4.2 %; shadow method "might be biased considering diverse topography" — [Annals of Forest Research 3014](https://afrjournal.org/index.php/afr/article/view/3014)
- Shadow geometry is used as an inverse check on point-cloud heights because field validation is labour-intensive — [Utah State AggieAir pubs 29](https://digitalcommons.usu.edu/aggieair_pubs/29)
- Advanced SfM workflows for crop height are reviewed, including point-cloud filtering and ground-model generation — [ScienceDirect S2352938525003817](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352938525003817)

### Inferences
- With the error floor at ~3.5 cm under ideal conditions and 6-8 cm for individual vegetables at typical heights, per-weed height for seedlings and rosettes (2-10 cm) is not recoverable from Swardus's nadir survey; a CHM could flag tall weeds (above ~20-30 cm, e.g. bolting thistle or pigweed above the crop) as a class, not a measurement.
- Shadow length is a legitimate single-frame geometry (sun elevation from timestamp + GPS, shadow length in pixels times GSD) but every published use is on trees; for a 5 cm weed on a 1-2 cm/px frame the shadow is a few pixels and merges with the plant's own dark edge. No source supports it for small plants.
- If height ever matters, the published route is a dedicated low (20-40 m) oblique flight, not re-use of nadir survey frames.

### Gaps
- No study reports CHM accuracy specifically for weeds or for plants under 10 cm; the smallest validated targets found are vegetable seedlings with 6-8 cm RMSE.
- No shadow-length validation on herbaceous plants was found.

## Key question 5: How do multi-frame studies decide which frame to measure a plant from, and how do they fuse?

### Takeaway
The published precedent (Phenoscript in the INRAE maize-density work) selects, per target, frames with full coverage, minimum blur and viewing direction closest to nadir, because near-nadir views have almost no plant-on-plant overlap; MatchPlant picks the minimum frame set that covers the area; phenotyping studies show the orthomosaic blends and warps pixels so measuring on original frames is more accurate, and that a plot appears in 10 or more frames with per-frame index values differing by up to 0.05 NDVI. No study was found that reports a formal per-plant voting or median fusion with accuracy against ground truth.

### Cited Findings
- Phenoscript "allows selecting, among the individual images available for each microplot, those with full coverage of the microplot, minimum blur and view direction closer to the nadir one"; near-nadir viewing "ensures that there is almost no overlap among individual plants"; frames were aligned in Photoscan and microplots extracted once ortho-rectified — [Velumani et al. 2021, arXiv 2105.11857](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.11857)
- MatchPlant's image-selection module "selects the minimum number of images necessary while maintaining full coverage", weighing flight-line width, overlap thresholds and uncovered-area percentage, from orthorectified images plus metadata; it does not use view angle — [MatchPlant, arXiv 2506.12295](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.12295)
- A single plot appears in "10 or more individual images" but once in the orthomosaic; NDVI from individual calibrated frames deviates by up to 0.05 from the orthomosaic value; the thesis compares estimating indices directly from all frames against the orthomosaic for lentil, wheat and canola — [Gray 2023, USask thesis, Beyond Orthomosaics](https://harvest.usask.ca/items/bcf20e2d-9837-4307-916c-a99f1667ea2e)
- Orthomosaic generation blends pixels in overlapping regions, changing values such as canopy temperature; extracting traits directly from orthorectified images improved accuracy — [PMC7609415](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7609415/)
- For palm detection, raw imagery gave higher accuracy for deployment while orthomosaic-trained models generalised better across domains — [arXiv 2509.12400](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.12400v2)
- A multi-angular framework extracts per-plant reflectance together with each view's zenith and azimuth angles from original frames and classifies maize plant parameters better than orthomosaic retrieval — [Precision Agriculture 2024, 10.1007/s11119-024-10133-0](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11119-024-10133-0)
- Object detection plus geometry-aware clustering is used to distinguish overlapping plants in UAV imagery — [arXiv 2609.21304](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21304)
- Image blur significantly affected SfM plant-height accuracy in sorghum — [Sensors Nov 2018 (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/10c10775975d4a5dbfb4a672e37dfa5f)
- Microplot localisation is normally done on the stitched image via hierarchical optimisation — [PMC8678615](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8678615/)

### Inferences
- A defensible Swardus rule, grounded in Phenoscript: for each weed spot from the map, list covering frames; drop frames where the spot is within a margin of the frame edge (lens distortion and off-nadir angle are worst there), rank the rest by off-nadir angle of the ray to the spot (computable from camera pose in the ODM reconstruction, not just the gimbal pitch), then by a sharpness score (Laplacian variance) and exposure sanity; measure on the top one or two frames.
- Fusion across frames is unsupported by published accuracy data; the literature's choice is "best frame" rather than averaging, because off-nadir views inflate projected area and shift the apparent centroid. If several near-nadir frames pass, the median of area across them is the conservative choice and the spread is an honest per-plant uncertainty to show.
- Re-projection of a frame mask into map coordinates is what every "orthorectified then extract" pipeline does implicitly; measuring in the frame (pixel count times per-frame GSD) avoids the mosaic's blend seams that PMC7609415 warns about.

### Gaps
- No study reports per-plant measurement accuracy as a function of off-nadir angle or of the number of frames fused.
- The USask thesis's full PDF (with its frame-aggregation method and RMSE comparisons) was not retrieved; only the abstract was readable.
- No paper found that applies blur or exposure rejection to per-plant measurement explicitly; blur rejection evidence is from SfM height work.

## Key question 6: Which datasets and benchmarks can serve for validation, and what are their licences (data and models)?

### Takeaway
For Swardus's use, the licence-clean UAV sets are WeedsGalore (maize, CC BY 4.0 data, Apache-2.0 code), CampanetaWeeds2 (orchard, six weed species, CC BY 4.0) and probably Sugar Beets 2016 (CC BY-SA 4.0, unconfirmed); PhenoBench carries a CC BY-SA 4.0 vs CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 conflict that must be settled with the authors before any commercial use; CropAndWeed is explicitly non-commercial; WeedMap's licence is not stated on its page. On the model side RF-DETR, SAM 2 and WeedsAreWeird are Apache-2.0 / MIT, YOLOv8 is AGPL-3.0, and ERFNet is CC BY-NC 4.0 with commercial licence by contacting the authors.

### Cited Findings
- PhenoBench: UAV sugar beet, 1 mm/px, 2,872 labelled + 129,000 unlabelled images, semantic / plant-instance / leaf-instance / detection tasks, hidden test set with server-side leaderboard — [arXiv 2306.04557v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.04557v2)
- PhenoBench licence: the Hugging Face card gives CC BY-SA 4.0 "per the official dataset page" (citing the 2024 T-PAMI paper) but the README bundled in PhenoBench-v110.zip (v1.1.0) states CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (citing the 2023 preprint); the card advises confirming with the authors for commercial use — [Voxel51/PhenoBench_Raw on Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Voxel51/PhenoBench_Raw); official site [phenobench.org](https://www.phenobench.org/) (403 this run)
- WeedMap: MicaSense RedEdge-M and Sequoia multispectral UAV imagery over sugar beet in Eschikon (CH) and Rheinbach (DE), ~1 cm GSD, 18,746 images across 129 directories in 8 datasets (000-007), classes background/crop/weed as colour and indexed label maps; no licence text on the page; DOI 10.3929/ethz-c-000788571 — [ETH ASL WeedMap page](https://projects.asl.ethz.ch/datasets/weedmap-2018/)
- CropAndWeed (AIT): custom non-commercial research licence; commercial use barred including redistribution — [Voxel51/CropAndWeed on Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Voxel51/CropAndWeed); [AIT dataset record](https://publications.ait.ac.at/en/datasets/cropandweed-dataset/)
- Sugar Beets 2016 (BoniRob, Chebrolu et al. IJRR 2017): official download at Bonn; Dataset Ninja lists CC BY-SA 4.0 while a GitHub mirror lists "other" — [Bonn Sugar Beets 2016 page](https://www.ipb.uni-bonn.de/data/sugarbeets2016); [Dataset Ninja](https://datasetninja.com/sugar-beets-2016)
- WeedsGalore (WACV 2025): UAV multispectral multitemporal maize, RGB + red-edge + NIR, dense annotations for maize and four weed classes, semantic and instance segmentation; data CC BY 4.0, code Apache-2.0 — [GitHub GFZ/weedsgalore](https://github.com/GFZ/weedsgalore); [arXiv 2502.13103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13103v1)
- CampanetaWeeds2 (Zenodo v2, Sept 2025): orchard (permanent crops), pixel-aligned RGB + four-band multispectral, instance-level annotations for six weed species, three flight acquisitions with flight logs, CC BY 4.0; GSD, image count and mask-vs-box not stated on the record — [Zenodo 17214769](https://zenodo.org/records/17214769)
- CWFID: 60 ground-robot carrot-field images, 492 labelled objects, intra- and inter-row weeds with vegetation masks — [Dataset Ninja CWFID](https://datasetninja.com/cwfid); [Haug & Ostermann ECCV 2014](https://mlanthology.org/eccv/2014/haug2014eccv-crop/)
- BAWSeg: UAV multispectral benchmark for barley weed segmentation — [arXiv 2603.01932](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.01932)
- GrowliFlower (UAV cauliflower) and SB20 (robot sugar beet, Bonn 2020) are used as cross-domain test sets in ZeroPlantSeg — [arXiv 2509.09116](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09116v1)
- A vineyard weed paper (CERTH 2023) covers intra-row vineyard weeds only — [CEUR-WS Vol-3293 paper 14](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3293/paper14.pdf)
- RF-DETR: Roboflow states core models and code are Apache 2.0 and free for commercial use (vendor statement) — [Roboflow blog](https://blog.roboflow.com/rf-detr-is-free-to-use-commercially/)
- YOLOv8: AGPL-3.0, with the copyleft extending to network deployment; Ultralytics sells enterprise licences — [Roboflow YOLOv8 instance segmentation page](https://playground.roboflow.com/models/ultralytics/yolov8-instance-segmentation)
- SAM 2: released July 2024 under Apache 2.0 (secondary source) — [Roboflow RF-DETR seg vs SAM 2](https://playground.roboflow.com/models/compare/rf-detr-segmentation-vs-sam-2)
- ERFNet (erfnet_pytorch): CC BY-NC 4.0, "personal and research use only", "for a commercial license please contact the authors"; the trained models in the repo appear to fall under the same terms — [GitHub Eromera/erfnet_pytorch](https://github.com/Eromera/erfnet_pytorch)
- WeedsAreWeird (Bonn SAM+BioCLIP zero-shot code): MIT — [GitHub PRBonn/WeedsAreWeird](https://github.com/PRBonn/WeedsAreWeird)

### Inferences
- For an orchard-first product, CampanetaWeeds2 is the only open orchard-floor weed set found and should be the first external check; WeedsGalore is the cleanest row-crop UAV set; PhenoBench stays research-only for Swardus until the authors confirm CC BY-SA.
- The ERFNet weights in the app are research-only (consistent with the existing licensing-audit note); any production path that keeps ERFNet needs a commercial licence from the authors or a swap to an Apache/MIT model.
- None of the open UAV sets is at 1-2 cm/px RGB nadir with instance masks for weeds in orchards; validation at Swardus's GSD will need Swardus's own annotated frames (the existing annotate.html / open-row benchmark is the right vehicle), with the public sets used for method sanity rather than headline accuracy.

### Gaps
- WeedMap licence not stated on the project page; the ETH DOI record was not checked.
- Fawakherji sunflower UAV weed dataset: no record or licence found.
- CampanetaWeeds2: GSD, platform, image count and whether annotations are masks or boxes are not on the Zenodo record; the dataset files would need to be opened.
- SAM 1 licence (Apache 2.0 is widely reported) was not verified from Meta's repository in this run.
- Sugar Beets 2016 licence is a third-party listing only.
