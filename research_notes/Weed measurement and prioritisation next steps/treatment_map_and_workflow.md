# Treatment map and workflow: what a drone weed-pressure map must become to be acted on

Scope note for the writer: this covers briefs (a) export formats and how they are consumed, (b) how commercial drone-weed products present pressure and what they charge, (c) validation practices and the lightest credible in-field accuracy display, (d) the post-map workflow (field walk, re-scan, patch memory), and (e) what practitioners say is missing. The five key questions below are the organising spine; (a) sits under Q1, (b) and (e) under Q2, (c) under Q3 and Q4, (d) under Q5. Research was done 2026-10-21. Several primary pages (GRDC update papers 2021/2023, Future Farming's spot-spraying Q&A, Farmonaut's cost model, the NDSU thesis page, the Izquierdo 2020 full text) returned HTTP 403; where I rely on a search snippet of those pages I say so.

## Q1. Which export formats would let a Turkish or European contractor actually spray from the map, and what are their resolution and polygon constraints?

### Takeaway
There are three practical consumers and three practical containers: spray drones (DJI Agras, XAG, TopXGun, EAVision, Talos, ABZ) take a WGS-84 polygon shapefile for a boundary mission and a shapefile + GeoTIFF raster for a variable-rate mission, with DJI's effective floor at a 1 m grid and a 4 to 5 m reaction distance; ground sprayers with section or nozzle control take a shapefile with a rate attribute (John Deere, Ag Leader, Trimble, Agrifac) or zipped ISOXML (ISO 11783-10 TASKDATA with a grid, used by Amazone Amatron 4, Hardi GeoSelect, Horsch, and any ISOBUS terminal); every vendor that has published a spot-spray recipe pads each weed cell with a buffer of 0.5 to 1 m and treats "spots" below roughly 1 m by 1 m as untreatable. A graded cell map at 0.5 m cells therefore already has the right geometry; what is missing is the rate column, the buffer, the merge into polygons, the WGS-84 shapefile and ISOXML writers, and a per-machine "minimum spot" setting.

### Cited Findings

Spray drones (DJI Agras, XAG and others)
- DJI's Agras app imports shapefiles with these rules (DJI support reply on the DJI forum, 2020-2021): only polygon geometry; clockwise rings are the plot edge and counter-clockwise rings are obstacles; a .shp and .dbf are required and the .dbf carries plot configuration (defaults used if absent); .shx is ignored; files go on the controller's microSD under DJI/ShapeFile. A user who got it working set the CRS to EPSG:4326 and geometry to Polygon in QGIS and reported different errors when either was not set — [DJI forum thread 224202](https://forum.dji.com/thread-224202-1-1.html)
- PIX4Dfields exports for DJI Agras T-series are a "boundary mission" (one DJI folder with a shapefile; each exported boundary becomes one flight mission) and a "VRA mission" (DJI folder with a Shapefile subfolder for the boundary and an Rx subfolder with a .tif of rates). Model support: boundary T10/T20/T30/T40/T50/T100; VRA T20/T30/T40/T50/T100 but not T10; multitask (several polygons flown consecutively) T50 and T25, boundary only. Rates cannot be edited on the controller. Import: SD card > Task > Import boundary; for VRA, Prescription Map > Map Source "Other", Source unit "ha", Resample "Max" or "Average". The controller's displayed rate and total volume "may be inaccurate after import" though the drone usually applies correctly, so Pix4D recommends a short test flight. Firmware floor for T-20P/T-30: aircraft and RC 01.00.0602, app 5.5.3 — [PIX4Dfields: DJI Agras T-series prescriptions](https://support.pix4d.com/hc/en-us/articles/360019956578)
- Pix4D's guidance on which DJI container to use for weeds: boundary mission "where one rate is applied, or where areas are very distinct (more than 3 hectares)"; VRA mission for "multiple rates or complex spot spraying", with the drawback that the operator must edit the boundary to avoid flying non-application areas (their example cut flight area from 15 ha to 12 ha) — [same Pix4D article](https://support.pix4d.com/hc/en-us/articles/360019956578)
- Grid floor and reaction distance for DJI: a Pix4D staff reply (Dec 2025) says "the minimum that DJI accepts is a 1-meter grid", which is also the smallest PIX4Dfields exports; the Agras "needs about 4 to 5 meters to react to a rate change"; choose the Max resample because when the drone reads two cells with different rates it applies the higher one; for VRA use a coarser grid so the drone "operates properly" — [Pix4D community: Grid size / DJI Agras T50](https://community.pix4d.com/t/grid-size-dji-agras-t50/34284)
- A T25 trial team mapped weeds on a 0.25 m grid, buffered spots by 1 m and separately by 4 m (their assumed working width), and found the herbicide quantity the drone demanded was far above Pix4D's area-based prediction; the explanation given was that DJI works on a 1 m x 1 m grid while the swath is much wider, so the drone oversamples, and that machines "have delays, internal buffers, and resample prescription maps"; the fix proposed was to plan on a grid sized to the swath — [Pix4D community: DJI Agras T25 for spot spraying, herbicide quantity](https://community.pix4d.com/t/dji-agras-t25-for-spotspraying-trouble-calculatiing-right-amount-of-herbizide-needed/34667)
- A T40 user reported a Pix4D shapefile importing as one mission per polygon, and that DJI Terra writes a .djitiles file "the only file type to be read by the remote as a prescription map" — [Pix4D community: Importing prescription map to Agras T40 remote](https://community.pix4d.com/t/importing-prescription-map-to-agras-t40-remote/24790)
- DJI SmartFarm (the web/app side) accepts a custom prescription as a TIF plus TFW world file under the Data Analysis tab (OneSoil help) — [OneSoil: VRA maps for DJI](https://help.onesoil.ai/en/articles/15819270-how-to-download-variable-rate-application-maps-for-dji)
- XAG: XAG announced (2024-10-30) that its drones read prescription maps from PIX4Dfields through the XAG One app; no file spec is given — [XAG news 213](https://xa.com/en/news/official/xag/213). Pix4D's brand table says XAG P100 boundary missions carry one fixed rate and VRA missions up to three rates — [PIX4Dfields: export a targeted spraying map](https://support.pix4d.com/hc/en-us/articles/11317942862109); the 2.8 release post introduced the XAG export — [Pix4D blog 2.8](https://www.pix4d.com/blog/pix4dfields-xag-prescriptions-AI-report/)
- Other spray-drone targets in Pix4D's table: TopXGun, Talos, EAVision all boundary (.shp) and VRA (.shp + .tiff); ABZ boundary and VRA; Hylio (Agrosol GCS) boundary only, one fixed rate, as GeoJSON — [PIX4Dfields export table](https://support.pix4d.com/hc/en-us/articles/11317942862109)
- Spray drones do zone spraying rather than spot spraying: "minimum zones of about 0.1 acre" (about 400 m2), versus ground sprayers with nozzle control that can treat areas "as small as 0.001 acre" (about 4 m2) — [Iowa State ICM, Houser, 2024-12-09](https://crops.extension.iastate.edu/post/drone-based-weed-mapping-targeted-spraying)

Ground sprayers: shapefile route
- John Deere: required files .shp .shx .dbf, optional .prj, placed in an "Rx" folder at the root of a USB stick — [VRAFY: John Deere 2630/Gen4/G5](https://vrafy.helpjuice.com/prescription-formatting/johndeere-2630-gen4-g5). On the Deere display a zero rate shows black and "zero rates in the prescription command sections OFF in Section Control", which is the mechanism that turns a prescription into a spot map — [Deere display help: Rx map](https://displaysimulator.deere.com/onscreen_help/4640/current/en/mapping/map_rx.htm)
- Ag Leader InCommand 800/1200: .shp .shx .dbf required, .prj optional, root or one folder deep — [VRAFY: Ag Leader](https://vrafy.helpjuice.com/prescription-formatting/agleader-prescription-uploading). A Pix4D forum post claims Ag Leader "doesn't support shape files that are small areas to turn on and off sections" (anecdotal, undated) — [Pix4D community: in-season weed ID, file compatibility](https://community.pix4d.com/t/weed-identification-in-season-spray-prescription-file-compatibility/25988)
- Climate FieldView prescription spec (Nov 2017, still the published one): zip containing exactly one .shp .shx .dbf; polygons or multipolygons only; EPSG:4326; no more than 100 zones per file; rate must be a non-negative real; one file per field — [FieldView developer: Rx format](https://dev.fieldview.com/rx-format)
- Agrifac: nozzles every 25 cm, each switched on and off from a loaded task map by GPS, "precision of 25 x 25 cm" — [Agrifac press release 2021](https://www.agrifac.com/app/uploads/2021/02/Agrifac_Pressrelease_Spot_spraying_Mona_Lisa_EN.pdf); Pix4D exports a shapefile "for Agrifac monitors" — [PIX4Dfields export table](https://support.pix4d.com/hc/en-us/articles/11317942862109)
- Horsch Leeb AutoSelect (map-based, no camera): Agrovista's drone maps use "typically a minimum of 1m grids"; two map styles exist, point-and-radius (centre of a weed population plus a circle, overlapping circular spray areas, "more sophisticated") and polygon ("fewer data points, more compatible with older control terminals"); Horsch UK calls it "patch-spraying" not spot spraying and says no one yet offers green-on-green spot spraying from maps — [Farm Contractor, 2025-06-02](https://www.farmcontractormagazine.com/farm-machinery/sprayers/horsch-offers-spot-spraying-without-an-expensive-camera-system.html). Pix4D's Horsch (eosT10Pro) export is a zipped shapefile with point geometry for spot spraying and zipped ISOXML for VRA — [PIX4Dfields export table](https://support.pix4d.com/hc/en-us/articles/11317942862109)

Ground sprayers: ISOXML / ISO 11783 route (the European contractor's terminal)
- ISOXML is ISO 11783-10 (current edition 2015; edition 3 is an approved work item). A prescription is a grid file inside the TASKDATA set; Type 1 grids are a byte array whose values point to a TreatmentZone, Type 2 grids are 32-bit integers holding the value directly and can hold multiple layers — [ISO 11783-10:2015](https://www.iso.org/ru/standard/61581.html); [Dev4Agriculture isoxml-dotnet README](https://github.com/dev4Agriculture/isoxml-dotnet); [NuGet package page](https://feed.nuget.org/packages/Dev4Agriculture.ISO11783.ISOXML/0.8.0)
- Pix4D's ISOXML targets: Amazone "SpotSpraying shapefile or zipped ISOXML, for Amatron 4 displays"; Hardi GeoSelect ".tiff or ISOXML (ISOBUS)"; Generic tractor/sprayer ".shp or .tiff, depending on equipment, or ISOXML" — [PIX4Dfields export table](https://support.pix4d.com/hc/en-us/articles/11317942862109)
- Amazone AmaSelect Spot (map-based spot spraying from drone RGB maps): 25 cm nozzle spacing, 50 cm section control, SpotFan 40-03 nozzles 50 cm above target, "treatment areas determined are extended by a 1 m wide safety zone", maps produced by a service provider (DroneWerkers, NL), savings "up to 80%" depending on weed cover — [Amazone press release](https://amazone.net/en/service-support/for-the-press/press-releases/press-archive-2019-/amaselect-spot-with-dronelink-45754). A French trade report (Nov 2025) says Amazone's GPS-Maps&Doc licence raises map resolution to up to one million distinct zones — [Pleinchamp](https://www.pleinchamp.com/actualite/pulverisation-ultra-localisee-les-cartes-a-1-million-de-point-d-amazone)
- SAM-DIMENSION (DE) delivers spot-spray maps "integrated into the ISOBUS sprayer terminal", onto "any Isobus-compatible sprayer with section control", within 24 h of flight; sub-2 mm GSD; no cell size or file name published — [CPM Magazine, 2026-04-29](https://www.cpm-magazine.co.uk/technology/a-drones-eye-view-on-weed-detection/); [Farmers Weekly, 2026-04-01](https://www.fwi.co.uk/machinery/spraying-and-fertilising/sprayers/sam-dimension-provides-ultra-accurate-weed-maps-for-sprayers)

Buffers, minimum spots and positioning
- Hohenheim on-farm trial (2024): weed boxes of 1 m x 1 m around each classified weed; smallest spot 1 m x 1 m; herbicide applied 0.5 m before and after each weed "to allow for nozzle reaction time"; Amazone UF 2002 with single-nozzle control, 0.5 m nozzle spacing, each nozzle 1 m spray width; maps loaded on the Amatron4 terminal (format unstated); flights 2 to 4 days before spraying — [Allmendinger et al., Plants 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11314123/)
- A Pix4D user runs 1 m cells, exports to QGIS, buffers each cell by 0.5 m and merges, "to account for sprayer guidance inaccuracies and off-target drift"; Pix4D agreed the option is needed (Feb 2024) but no built-in buffer was shipped in that thread — [Pix4D community: buffers around spot cells](https://community.pix4d.com/t/feature-request-creating-buffers-around-spot-spraying-cells/29272)
- Another user runs "a 1.5m cell + the cell buffer. Any smaller and I can start to see boom sections not fire and keep up" and asks for 1 m x 4.5 m rectangles along the drive direction to spray less area than squares (search snippet; thread fetch was refused) — [Pix4D community: Rectangle grid](https://community.pix4d.com/t/rectangle-grid/34097)
- Sentera + Capstan nozzle control (2025): "7-10-foot minimum length spray zones on most sprayer systems"; with the Capstan module, zones "as small as a few square feet" and 1.5 ft in 2025 trials; 97% hit rate at 12 and 18 mph on a 2012 RoGator — [Sentera, 2025-04-20](https://sentera.com/2025/04/20/unlocking-the-power-of-precision-how-sentera-and-capstan-ag-are-transforming-targeted-weed-control/)
- Pix4D: use the same RTK NTRIP network for the mapping drone and the spraying machine or "large location errors are possible"; if no internet, a local base station reused at the same spot — [PIX4Dfields: RTK/NTRIP](https://support.pix4d.com/hc/en-us/articles/36002011123123). Without corrections "GPS error of 1 to 3 meters makes large-acre weed pickup unworkable" (RTK vendor claim) — [UST feature on RTKdata](https://www.unmannedsystemstechnology.com/feature/rtkdata-supports-precision-agricultural-drone-mapping-across-the-upper-midwest/)
- PWM: flow is varied by solenoid duty cycle at constant pressure; commercial systems blend pulses so alternate nozzles fire on opposite phases to avoid skips — [UGA Extension C1277](https://fieldreport.caes.uga.edu/publications/C1277/pulse-width-modulation-technology-for-agricultural-sprayers). Per-nozzle switching at very fine grids is limited by ISOBUS bandwidth ("way too high for existing Isobus communication protocols") — [Future Farming, PWM plant-per-plant](https://futurefarming.com/crop-solutions/weed-pest-control/how-to-vra-plant-per-plant-with-pwm-technology)

### Inferences
- For Swardus the minimum viable set is: (1) a WGS-84 polygon shapefile (.shp/.shx/.dbf/.prj) of treat/no-treat polygons with a numeric rate attribute, which reaches DJI Agras boundary missions, John Deere, Ag Leader, Trimble, Agrifac, Horsch polygon mode and FieldView; (2) a GeoTIFF + TFW of the same cells (1 m cells, Max resample) for DJI/XAG/TopXGun VRA missions; (3) zipped ISOXML TASKDATA with a Type 1 or Type 2 grid for Amazone/Hardi/Horsch/any ISOBUS terminal. Point-and-radius (Horsch) is a fourth, cheap to add from patch centroids.
- The 0.5 m analysis grid should never be exported raw: the published spot-map recipes all dilate (0.5 to 1 m), merge to polygons, then drop or expand anything below the machine's minimum (about 1 m x 1 m for nozzle-control booms, 7 to 10 ft lengths for ordinary section control, about 0.1 acre zones for spray drones). "Buffer", "minimum spot" and "drive direction" are machine settings the operator should pick per export.
- Because Swardus does not give product rates, the rate attribute should carry application volume (L/ha) or a 1/0 treat flag, with zero meaning sections off (Deere semantics). That stays inside the decision-support rule.
- The T25 volume mismatch and the "controller shows wrong total" warning mean the export screen must state treated area in hectares and warn that the machine's own total will differ; a map that promises litres invites the exact complaint seen in the Pix4D forum.
- Keep the field boundary and any obstacles as separate rings (clockwise outer, counter-clockwise holes) because DJI reads ring direction as semantics.

### Gaps
- No public ISO 11783-10 text was reachable, so the exact grid limits (max rows/columns, 254/255 treatment-zone limit for Type 1, GridCellSize minimums) are not confirmed here; they must be checked in the standard or in the Dev4Agriculture library source before writing an ISOXML exporter.
- XAG's own file specification (extension, raster vs vector, cell floor) was not found; only Pix4D's "up to three rates" statement.
- No official DJI document lists Agras shapefile/prescription constraints; the 1 m grid floor and 4 to 5 m reaction distance are Pix4D staff experience, not DJI specification.
- No Turkish source on prescription-map use with spray drones was found; the Turkish market is assumed to mirror DJI/XAG import rules.

## Q2. What do farmers reportedly do with weed maps today, and where do current tools fail them?

### Takeaway
Commercially, weed maps are sold as a service (Taranis, Sentera, Agrovista, SAM-DIMENSION, DroneWerkers) that returns a colour-coded pressure or density map with species and a one-click zone-to-prescription export, mostly as a shapefile for the cab; growers who do it themselves (Pix4Dfields Magic Tool, Skippy Scout) report that the detection step is slow and manual, that buffers and machine-aware grids are missing, that predicted herbicide volume does not match what the machine demands, and that maps are not yet an export into the sprayer at all (Skippy Scout, Feb 2024). Independent studies add that drone maps undercount small and shaded weeds badly, so the map is trusted for "where the patches are", not for counts.

### Inferences
- Every service product converges on the same five artefacts: a colour-coded pressure map, species list, auto zones the operator can edit, a rate-per-zone form, and a shapefile download or Operations Center push within 24 h. Swardus already has the first; the "zones you can edit, then export" step is the missing product surface, not more detection.
- The DIY tools fail at the seams: training the classifier by hand, buffering, matching the grid to the machine, and volume prediction. A tool that ships with sensible defaults (1 m cells, 0.5 to 1 m buffer, machine-specific minimum spot, treated hectares stated plainly) removes the three most-repeated forum complaints.
- The literature and the WA grower agree that the map's trustworthy content is patch location, not weed count; presenting counts invites the Montana finding (78 to 85% undercount) as a complaint. Grading cover and listing patches is the defensible framing.
- Pricing in this market runs from £30/month (Skippy) through USD 499 to 3,999/yr (Sentera tiers) and USD 1,650 to 2,450/yr (Agremo) to service plans with no published price (Taranis, Sentera SmartScript, Agrovista, SAM-DIMENSION); a per-hectare contract price of about AUD 4 to 7/ha is the only public service benchmark.

### Gaps
- No 2025 or 2026 price for PIX4Dfields, Taranis AcreForward or Sentera SmartScript was found; vendor pages hide amounts behind sales.
- No farmer-forum threads (AgTalk, Reddit) surfaced through the search tool; practitioner complaints here come from vendor forums (Pix4D), trade press and extension articles.
- Carbon Bee's own drone-map savings video and Bilberry's independent results were not retrievable; Solvi and DroneDeploy show no published weed-detection feature at all.
- Taranis facts beyond the memory file are limited to the KB export flow and the 2026 yield-impact launch items; no new resolution or price was found.

## Q3. How much herbicide do site-specific studies and products report saving, and at what accuracy?

### Takeaway
Independent trials cluster at roughly 45 to 50% herbicide saved for early, map-based spot spraying with control and yield equal to broadcast (Hohenheim 47% with 86% efficacy; Iowa State about 46 to 50% with 94% of weeds dead and yield within 1.5 bu/ac), while camera-on-sprayer systems and vendor claims run 80 to 97%; savings fall sharply with late timing (12%) and with high weed pressure, and detection accuracy that sounds high at the orthomosaic level (94%) coexists with 41 to 85% omission of individual weeds against a ground camera.

### Cited Findings
- Hohenheim on-farm maize trial, four fields, 2023, 50 m / 1.4 mm GSD RGB with RTK: classifier missed 1.5% of weeds with 4.5% false positives (average "94%"), validated by manually marking weeds in ArcGIS on the same imagery (no quadrats, no per-field breakdown); early spot spraying saved 47% herbicide (about 50% if misclassifications are discounted) with 86% efficacy, statistically equal to early broadcast (76%); pre + early spot 44% saving but 63% efficacy; late spot 12% saving and 20% efficacy; yields not different (44.6 to 50.7 t/ha silage); classification only worked to about the four-leaf stage of maize — [Allmendinger et al., Plants 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11314123/)
- Iowa State demonstration (Sentera AWS, Deere ExactApply, 8.7 acres soybean, 2024): 70 gal tank mix vs 130.5 gal for 15 gal/ac broadcast (about 46%, article says about 50%), $13.42/acre saved; 118 weeds counted, 117 injured and 111 (94%) dead at 17 days; yield within 1.5 bu/ac, not significant; "the lower the weed pressure, the higher the savings"; ISU's separate five-field ground-camera study gave 44 to 91%; Deere reported 59% average See & Spray savings in 2024 — [ISU ICM, Houser, 2024-12-09](https://crops.extension.iastate.edu/post/drone-based-weed-mapping-targeted-spraying)
- Montana State (fallow, 2025): omission vs camera sprayer 41 to 65% (ExG), 48 to 74% (NIR), 78 to 85% (YOLO); no savings figure reported — [MSU Aug 2026](https://agresearch.montana.edu/narc/Programs_and_projects/narc-precisionag/newsletterprecisionagupdate/august2026.html)
- Black-grass in 26 UK wheat fields at 100 m / 3.2 cm GSD, ground truth by trained observers in 20 x 20 m plots scored in five density bands: within-field R2 0.29 (RGB) to 0.41, best field 0.46; presence/absence AUC 0.88 (accuracy 0.68); field-to-field transfer correlation 0.34; authors recommend treating UAS maps as "signposts" directing targeted field walking — [Lambert et al., Weed Research 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5832304/)
- Cotton weed-mapping study with five 1 m x 1 m quadrats per plot: image cover vs ground density r2 0.80 overall, up to 0.91 for Palmer amaranth; occurrence accuracy 89/86/83% with kappa 0.84/0.79/0.75 in high/medium/low density plots — [AgriEngineering 2020](https://www.mdpi.com/2624-7402/2/2/24)
- Maize random-forest mapping: 5-fold CV overall accuracy 0.945, kappa 0.912, detected-vs-ground density linear fit R2 0.895, RMSE 0.026 (aggregator page of a journal article) — [GIS Resources summary](https://gisresources.com/?p=1798)
- Vendor and service claims: Amazone AmaSelect Spot "up to 80%" — [Amazone](https://amazone.net/en/service-support/for-the-press/press-releases/press-archive-2019-/amaselect-spot-with-dronelink-45754); SAM-DIMENSION "up to 70% on average, up to 90%" — [CPM 2026](https://www.cpm-magazine.co.uk/technology/a-drones-eye-view-on-weed-detection/); Pix4D peanut case 93% input reduction — [drone.ua](https://drone.ua/en/blogs/stati/pix4dfields-helps-to-detect-weeds-in-peanut-fields); CultiWise 25 cm trial estimate 97% less chemical — [profi](https://www.profi.co.uk/test-centre/cultiwise-mini-and-scout-mapping-fields-with-drones-on-the-spot/); Greeneye 87 to 94% (UNL trial) — [PR Newswire](https://prnewswire.com/il/news-releases/greeneye-precision-spraying-system-reduces-non-residual-herbicide-use-by-87-while-delivering-same-efficacy-as-broadcast-spraying-unl-trial-finds-899331097.html); Bilberry 97.5% / Trimble "up to 90%" — [Farms.com](https://www.farms.com/precision-agriculture-digest/2024/spring/5/); Precision AI 83% (own trial) — [Brownfield](https://www.brownfieldagnews.com/news/precision-ai-advancing-drone-capabilities/); Sentera 94% detection at 1/4 inch across 13,000+ acres (own claim) — [Farm Progress](https://farmprogress.pl.barchart.com/mobile/news/story/31428290); eBee X case 39% — [Eagle NXT case studies](https://eaglenxt.com/?p=100456); WA grower 60 to 90% — [Farm Weekly](https://www.farmweekly.com.au/story/9297804/this-graingrowers-strategy-to-improve-crop-health-and-save-money/); Skippy Scout "up to £44/ha" — [Drone Ag wiki](https://droneag.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SS/pages/1650884609)
- Economic caution from the patch literature: wild-oat simulations in Spain found site-specific treatment "not always more profitable than uniform spraying, depending on yield losses" — [UCO repository record](https://helvia.uco.es/xmlui/handle/10396/17926?show=full)

### Inferences
- The honest number for a map-based program is "about half the herbicide at equal control, if sprayed early and if pressure is moderate"; the 80 to 97% figures belong to camera sprayers, green-on-brown fallow, or vendor case studies. Savings scale with the share of the field below threshold, which is exactly what a graded cover map shows; Swardus can report "treated area X ha of Y ha" and let the saving follow, without promising a percentage.
- Accuracy claims that survive scrutiny are stated as recall of a defined thing (weed presence per quadrat or per cell, kappa, omission vs a ground reference) at a stated growth stage and GSD; "94%" without a denominator is the pattern the Montana study punctures.
- Timing is a bigger lever than detection quality: late maps lose most of the saving and the efficacy. A product should show map age and crop stage next to the grade.

### Gaps
- Percent of field treated is rarely reported alongside savings (Hohenheim does not; ISU does not); only the Kansas anecdote (80 to 30 acres) and the Pix4D DJI example (15 to 12 ha flight area) give area figures.
- No independent trial of a drone map in an orchard or perennial crop with savings figures was found; all quantitative trials are row crops or fallow.

## Q4. What is the lightest credible way to show accuracy on the operator's own field?

### Takeaway
The literature's unit of trust is the 1 m2 quadrat (or a 20 x 20 m density-banded plot for sparse grass weeds), scored by a person and compared to the map cell; products instead earn trust by showing the source photo next to the mark (Sentera's phone app, Skippy Scout's automatic close-up flight to the suspected patch, then a post-spray re-fly). The lightest credible in-app form is a random audit of graded cells with the original frame shown at native resolution, the operator's agree/disagree stored, and the running agreement rate shown per grade class; this is a quadrat survey with the camera standing in for the walk.

### Cited Findings
- Validation designs in the literature: five 1 m x 1 m quadrats per plot, overall accuracy plus kappa, and a density regression (r2, RMSE) — [AgriEngineering 2020](https://www.mdpi.com/2624-7402/2/2/24); 20 x 20 m plots scored absent/low/medium/high/very high by three trained observers for black-grass, with the finding that a model from one field "rarely performs well when applied elsewhere" so local ground truth is needed — [Lambert et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5832304/); 5-fold cross-validation with kappa and a detected-vs-truth regression — [GIS Resources summary](https://gisresources.com/?p=1798); ground truth by marking weeds manually on the same high-resolution imagery, which is what Hohenheim did instead of quadrats — [Allmendinger et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11314123/); comparison against a sprayer-mounted camera as the reference, reported as omission rate per date — [MSU 2026](https://agresearch.montana.edu/narc/Programs_and_projects/narc-precisionag/newsletterprecisionagupdate/august2026.html)
- Product practice: Sentera shows "weed locations viewable on mobile devices with position information for in-field scouting" — [Sentera pricing/feature page](https://sentera.com/pricing/); the Kansas co-op validates by "examining the photos on a phone app" before loading the shapefile — [Corteva](https://www.corteva.com/us/Resources/crop-protection/soybeans/drones-valuable-weed-control-tool.html); Skippy Scout marks Scout Points on the suspected patch, flies a close-up capture to confirm, then re-flies after the weeds die "to verify the spray worked" — [Drone Ag wiki](https://droneag.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SS/pages/1650884609); Agrovista's operators hand-flag two or three weed areas before the AI generalises, a human-in-the-loop seed step — [Farm Contractor](https://www.farmcontractormagazine.com/farm-machinery/sprayers/horsch-offers-spot-spraying-without-an-expensive-camera-system.html)
- Post-spray checks as the accuracy proof growers accept: ISU counted 118 weeds before and 111 dead at 17 days — [ISU ICM](https://crops.extension.iastate.edu/post/drone-based-weed-mapping-targeted-spraying); the Kansas case reports "full weed control on a later check" — [Corteva](https://www.corteva.com/us/Resources/crop-protection/soybeans/drones-valuable-weed-control-tool.html)
- Efficacy from imagery: drone vegetation indices correlated 0.64 overall (NDVI 0.67) with visual weed-control ratings, best at 7 and 14 days after application — [Iowa State: UAV indices vs efficacy ratings](https://dr.lib.iastate.edu/entities/publication/1a5a038b-0c44-4334-94fa-8f0c80d6f0c8)

### Inferences
- A "random cell audit" screen (N random cells per grade class, original frame crop at native pixels beside the grade, agree/disagree, running agreement by class) reproduces the quadrat method with the operator as observer and needs no benchmark table; the number to show is "of the cells you checked, X of Y agreed", per class, with the date. This matches the founder's stated trust in what is drawn over imagery.
- Because transfer between fields is poor (Lambert), per-field audits are not optional polish; they are what the literature says is required for any field the model has not seen.
- Seeding the classifier from two or three operator-marked patches (Agrovista's practice) is a light-weight way to make the audit also improve the map, without a training pipeline.
- The second half of credibility is the post-spray re-scan at 7 to 14 days: show the same cells before and after and let the operator see the dead patches; this is the only accuracy display growers in the case studies actually cite.

### Gaps
- No product documents a formal random-audit UI; this is an inference from the quadrat literature plus Sentera/Skippy practice.
- No published guidance on how many cells per class make an audit credible; the literature uses 5 quadrats per plot or 3 observers per 20 x 20 m plot, which does not translate directly to a 0.5 m cell grid.

## Q5. What is the state of the art for re-scan comparison and patch memory across seasons?

### Takeaway
Re-scan after treatment is practised informally (Skippy Scout's "verify the spray worked" re-fly, ISU's 17-day count, Queensland DPI's pre-map/treat/assess cycle) and imagery-based efficacy correlates moderately with visual ratings at 7 to 14 days; season-over-season patch memory rests on a mixed literature: perennial and grass-weed patches persist and recur near old patches, annual broadleaf patches move, so a stored patch layer is a prior for where to look and a pre-emergence target, not a prescription to reuse blind.

### Cited Findings
- Re-scan practice: Skippy Scout's documented loop ends with a full Sphere plus scouting flight after the weeds die "to verify the spray worked" — [Drone Ag wiki](https://droneag.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SS/pages/1650884609); Queensland DPI reprocessed a drone dataset to assess several herbicides on Aleman grass and concluded the full cycle of pre-mapping, treating and assessing by drone was valid — [Qld DPI, PAWS 2025](https://era.dpi.qld.gov.au/id/eprint/15081/1/Utilising%20unmanned%20aerial%20vehicles_SetterS_p169PAWS2025.pdf); ISU drone-index vs visual rating correlation 0.64, best at 7 and 14 DAA — [Iowa State](https://dr.lib.iastate.edu/entities/publication/1a5a038b-0c44-4334-94fa-8f0c80d6f0c8); ISU demo: before/after count at 17 days — [ISU ICM](https://crops.extension.iastate.edu/post/drone-based-weed-mapping-targeted-spraying)
- Navigation and offline use for the field walk: Taranis Scout lets the user prepare fields in advance, navigate scouting missions offline and sync later — [Taranis KB](https://knowledge.taranis.ag/portal/en/kb/articles/offline-scoutin); Trimble Connected Farm Scout uses phone GPS with vegetation maps in the background to navigate to struggling areas — [Trimble developer listing](https://agdeveloper.trimble.com/?p=935); AgroScout "navigate to each infected location, much like a road navigation app" — [PotatoPro](https://potatopro.com/node/109471); Skippy Scout's results need "internet coverage" in the field to arrive in minutes and the live-GPS field layer was still "coming soon" (Feb 2024) — [Drone Ag wiki](https://droneag.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SS/pages/1650884609)
- Patch stability across seasons: Rozenberg, Carmel, Eshel and Blank (Pest Management Science, online 2025-12-08, DOI 10.1002/ps.70400), ten plots in northern Israel over 2021-2022: aggregation higher under conservation agriculture, perennials formed denser patches than annuals, and "weed patch stability was mainly governed by species life form, with perennials demonstrating persistent patches" — [Technion PDF](https://ecologylab.net.technion.ac.il/files/2026/05/Gal-Rozenberg-et-al-Pest-Management-Science-2025-Weed-spatial-patterns.pdf); Izquierdo et al. 2020 (Catalan cereals, three years): patches stable over time in only two harrow-tilled fields with Lolium and one direct-drilled field with Bromus, not in the others, so patches "would have to be identified afresh in each season" even though aggregation justifies site-specific control — [Agronomy 2020](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4395/10/4/452); [Rothamsted record](https://repository.rothamsted.ac.uk/id/eprint/30549); Clay et al., 65 ha corn/soybean field 1995-2004: grass patches "more stable in time, space, and density than common ragweed patches" — [SDSU Open Prairie](https://openprairie.sdstate.edu/plant_faculty_pubs/162); UNL extension: patch distribution "generally stable across years, although this depends somewhat on the species present and management practices", tied to the seed bank — [UNL EC708](https://extensionpubs.unl.edu/publication/ec708/na/pdf/view); wild oat in Spain: persistence and generalised spread with new patches appearing near old ones — [UCO record](https://helvia.uco.es/xmlui/handle/10396/17926?show=full); purple nutsedge: stable patches with fluctuating margins — [Iranian J. Weed Science](https://ijws.areeo.ac.ir/article_1214_bfab2f68df4262ce6fea9937f69cd757.pdf)
- A grower's stated use of last season's map: enlarge the mapped blackgrass area for "a targeted pre-emergence spray" the next season — [Farmers Weekly, two farmers](https://www.fwi.co.uk/arable/2-arable-farmers-explain-use-drones); the same article's second farmer asks for "a mapping history database".
- Lambert et al. list "test year-on-year transferability" as open work and frame maps as signposts for walking — [Lambert et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5832304/)

### Inferences
- The lightest credible re-scan feature is a same-cell before/after view at 7 to 14 days with the grade difference drawn, not a new metric: ISU's correlation says indices are only a moderate proxy, and the operator's eye on the paired frames is the decisive check.
- Patch memory should store patch outlines per season with life-form (the operator's own verdict on perennial vs annual) and present them next season as "last year's patches" for the field walk and for widening a pre-emergence target, in line with Izquierdo's finding that patches must be re-identified each season and Rozenberg's that perennials persist. In a young orchard, where the weed flora is often perennial between rows, stored patches are likely to be worth more than in annual row crops.
- Offline is a real differentiator: Skippy requires in-field internet for results, Taranis supports offline navigation only inside its closed service; a cached orthomosaic tile set plus patch list plus source frames on the phone covers the rural, low-connectivity user the mission names.

### Gaps
- No quantitative patch-overlap percentages across years were extractable from the Rozenberg PDF (text not readable by the fetch tool); only the abstract-level conclusion is cited.
- No study was found that measures how old a drone weed map can be before spraying from it fails (map age vs control); the Hohenheim trial sprayed 2 to 4 days after flight, Sentera delivers within 24 h, and that is the only timing evidence.
- No published offline-first scouting workflow for self-flown drone maps was found; the offline examples are closed services.
