# Grade patches, measure plants, deliver machine-ready maps

Swardus should keep its 0.5 m grid for drawing but stop deciding on it: every published cover threshold it uses (the Turkish ministry's 10–15 %, the German 9–10 %) was derived at plot or orchard-mean scale, and every drone study that applies a percentage threshold does so on cells of 1 m × row-width or larger, so the fix for "one 20 cm weed paints a red cell" is to compute cover in a moving window the size of the treatment unit (the 1–1.5 m tree strip in 1–3 m lengths for a young orchard; nozzle-section width × 1–3 m along the row for corn, soybean, wheat and cotton; never above 6 × 6 m) and to show a lone plant as a sized "watch" mark unless it is a rhizomatous perennial, past the size a label controls, or flowering ([Peña et al. 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3795646); [TAGEM olive guide](https://www.tarimorman.gov.tr/TAGEM/Belgeler/Entegre/Zeytin%20Entegre%20M%C3%BCcadele%20Teknik%20Talimat%C4%B1.pdf); [Paice et al. 1998](https://repository.rothamsted.ac.uk/item/87y3q/a-stochastic-simulation-model-for-evaluating-the-concept-of-patch-spraying)). The kept 1–2 cm/px source photographs can honestly measure one thing per weed, projected canopy area and the diameter derived from it (weeds under about 3 px, so 3–4 cm at 1 cm/px and 6–8 cm at 2 cm/px, are below the floor), plus a flowering flag from hue; height, biomass, BBCH leaf stage and named species are not recoverable at this pixel size, since every published species-level drone result sits at 0.09–0.32 cm/px and even handheld apps score 11–67 % fully correct on weeds ([Huang et al. 2018 review of Peña 2015](https://ars.usda.gov/ARSUserFiles/60663500/Publications/Huang/2018/Huang%20et%20al_2018_WT_32-2-6.pdf); [AgWeb app test](https://www.agweb.com/news/crops/crop-production/are-you-spraying-right-weeds-apps-can-help)). The classical chain (ExGR with Otsu, a shadow mask, marker-controlled watershed, best-frame selection by nadir angle and sharpness) covers the measurement; SAM 2 (Apache-2.0) earns a place only as a boundary refiner prompted by an index blob, and the one orchard dataset with a clean licence for checking any of it is CampanetaWeeds2 (CC BY 4.0). Prioritisation should be a visible lexical ranking (species class, then cover at unit scale × patch area, then strip-versus-alley, then stage, then history), not a hidden score, and the output must leave the app as buffered, merged polygons sized to the machine (1 m minimum spot, 0.5–1 m buffer, 1 m grid floor for DJI Agras) in WGS-84 shapefile, GeoTIFF+TFW and ISOXML, with treated hectares stated and no product rate. Independent trials put the honest saving for early map-based spraying at about 47–50 % at equal control, falling to 12 % when late, so timing and map age belong on the screen next to the grade ([Allmendinger et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11314123/); [Iowa State ICM 2024](https://crops.extension.iastate.edu/post/drone-based-weed-mapping-targeted-spraying)).

## Thresholds belong to the treatment unit, not the 0.25 m² cell

The reason today's map lights single plants is arithmetic, not detection. A weed 20 cm across has a projected canopy of roughly 0.03 m²; in a 0.25 m² cell that is about 12 % cover, which already clears the 10 % action point the grader takes from the Turkish ministry's olive and apricot rule. The same plant is about 3 % of a 1 m² unit and under 0.4 % of a 3 × 3 m one, while a genuine patch keeps its cover as the window grows. This is the general "modifiable areal unit" effect the GIS literature documents: extreme values are averaged out as aggregation units grow, and the recommended practice is to test several unit sizes and offsets rather than trust one ([UCGIS Body of Knowledge](https://gistbok-ltb.ucgis.org/current/print/concept/FC-07-026)). The operator's eye already applies this averaging when he looks at the imagery; the grader does not.

The published thresholds were never meant for a quarter-square-metre cell. The Turkish olive IPM guide samples with 50 × 50 cm frames, ten frames for 1–5 decares up to thirty for over 50 decares, and decides to treat when **the orchard mean reaches 10–15 weeds per m² or 10–15 % cover**; the threshold is applied to the sample mean over 2.5–7.5 m² of ground, not per frame ([TAGEM olive guide, pp. 14–15](https://www.tarimorman.gov.tr/TAGEM/Belgeler/Entegre/Zeytin%20Entegre%20M%C3%BCcadele%20Teknik%20Talimat%C4%B1.pdf)). The German economic thresholds of **9.2–9.8 % absolute cover for wheat and 4.5–8.9 % for barley** come from 122 plot-scale trials ([Keller et al. 2014](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10343-014-0335-8)). In the drone literature, Peña's maize map used cells of **1 m along the row by the 0.7 m inter-row**, graded weed-free, under 5 %, 5–20 % and over 20 %, and stated that the grid "can be customized on the basis of cropping patterns and the specifications required by the herbicide spraying machinery" ([Peña et al. 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3795646)). The two studies that did use 0.5 m cells both chose that size because the sprayer's nozzles were 0.5 m apart, and both then paired it with an "any weed" rule rather than a percentage: Huang's rice work recommended a cover threshold of zero "because it minimizes the risk of missed weeds" (savings rose only from 58 % to 71 % as the threshold went from 0 to 0.25), and the NDSU corn trial sprayed any cell holding one whole weed, later judged its 0.5 m × 3 m cells to have hurt efficacy, and said a roughly 3 × 3 m cell "would have been useful" ([Huang et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6209949); [NDSU, arXiv 2206.01734](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.01734)). Percentage threshold at 0.5 m is the one combination nobody published, and it is the one Swardus runs.

The upper bound on the unit is economic. Paice's long-term blackgrass simulation treated squares "whose size was set by the sprayer's spatial resolution" and concluded patch spraying "is unlikely to be profitable in the long term if the treated control area exceeds 6 m x 6 m" ([Paice et al. 1998](https://repository.rothamsted.ac.uk/item/87y3q/a-stochastic-simulation-model-for-evaluating-the-concept-of-patch-spraying)). The lower bound is the machine and the resistance literature together: classic boom sections are 3–5 m wide and individual nozzle control gives 50 cm sections ([Amazone](https://amazone.net/en/products-digital-solutions/electric-individual-nozzle-control-with-actual-50-cm-part-width-section-control-56820)), narrow sections with triangular-pattern nozzles increase under-dosed area on small dispersed patches ([Villette et al. 2019](https://hal.inrae.fr/hal-02609786)), and a 2026 spatially explicit simulation found that more accurate spot treatment "selected more strongly for resistance" while buffer zones around detections cut the share of escapes ([Salinas et al. 2026](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/do-spatially-targeted-weed-management-strategies-increase-risk-of-herbicide-resistance-evolution/)). A buffered unit of one to a few square metres is therefore defensible on three independent grounds: it is where the thresholds were measured, it is what the machine can execute, and razor-precise treatment is the stronger resistance selector.

For the apricot orchard the unit has a natural shape. Young-tree guidance converges on a weed-free band of **0.8–1.5 m from the trunk for one-to-three-year-old peach**, 1–2 m in Australian horticulture, and "at least three feet" in US practice, with the alley either mown or sown to cover ([NCSU peach strip trial](https://repository.lib.ncsu.edu/items/338fcd5c-7376-42f6-ad8d-a5e746003215); [HIN weed management](https://www.hin.com.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0017/18017/Weed-management.pdf); [Good Fruit Grower](https://goodfruit.com/careful-weed-control-is-critical-in-new-orchards)); Madrid olive research keeps a 0.5 m herbicide strip each side of the tree line and mows the alley before it competes for water ([WOCAT 1174](https://qcat.wocat.net/en/wocat/technologies/view/permalink/1174/)). So the orchard window is the strip band (about 1–1.5 m either side of the trunk line, which the planting layout already fixes) in 1–3 m lengths, with the alley graded and reported as cover but not marked "treat here" unless the operator has declared a bare floor or the patch is a listed perennial. For the row crops the window is the operator's nozzle-section width across rows by 1–3 m along them. In both cases the 0.5 m cell can remain the step of the window, so the drawing does not change its grain, only its meaning: a cell turns red when the window centred on it passes the threshold, and an isolated plant that would have been red becomes a small pin carrying its measured size.

One honest caveat: no study justifies a cell size from patch statistics in a drone-surveyed orchard; all the numbers above are row-crop or simulation, and the grid sizes of two often-cited sunflower and maize studies could not be read behind paywalls ([López-Granados et al. 2016](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s11119-015-9415-8); [Castaldi et al. 2017](https://iris.cnr.it/handle/20.500.14243/327815)). The product should expose the unit as a setting with a sensible default and let the operator see the map change as he moves it, which is also what the MAUP guidance asks for.

## Source frames measure area and diameter honestly; height, biomass and leaf stage they do not

At 1–2 cm per pixel the floor is set by pixel count, not by the algorithm. Peña's resolution study found that a 3 cm seedling in a 5.4 cm pixel becomes a mixed pixel and "weed objects may not be created", and the review of it puts individual-plant discrimination at **1–4 cm pixels** with patches tolerating 5 cm or coarser ([Peña et al. 2015](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/273139881_Quantifying_Efficacy_and_Limits_of_Unmanned_Aerial_Vehicle_UAV_Technology_for_Weed_Seedling_Detection_as_Affected_by_Sensor_Resolution); [Huang et al. 2018](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/weed-technology/article/uav-lowaltitude-remote-sensing-for-precision-weed-management/6C81EEBF460FF0FC9E48F51E331425C5)). Scaled to Swardus's frames, a weed needs roughly three pixels across to be separated from soil, so 3–4 cm at 1 cm/px and 6–8 cm at 2 cm/px; a 10 cm weed is comfortably segmentable, a 5 cm one is marginal. Models trained at finer pixels do not rescue this: on PhenoBench at 1 mm/px, weed IoU is 64 against crop IoU 94 and weed detection AP 17–38 against crop AP 60–83, with the authors blaming "barely visible small plants" ([PhenoBench, arXiv 2306.04557](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.04557v2)). Tiny weeds are out of scope at this altitude by physics, which matches the product's existing decision.

For the weeds that are resolvable, the light chain the literature supports is a colour index with a sensible threshold, a shadow correction, and careful markers. ExG with Otsu reaches **87.5 % pixel accuracy** for canopy cover and ExG minus ExR with a tuned threshold **94.7 %** ([Univ. of Tehran](https://journals.ut.ac.ir/article_87641_dd04186b1426cc39291f08aba93025c8.pdf)); a UAV pipeline segmented green plants at 93.5 % with ExGR and minimum-error thresholding before splitting crop from weed ([Remote Sensing 2021](https://doaj.org/article/40849e1dd13b459d8c171355b153585e)). ExG "often performs poorly when dark shadows create strong illumination differences", so an illumination-invariant transform is the published fix ([WUR shadow-resistant segmentation](https://research.wur.nl/en/publications/shadow-resistant-segmentation-based-on-illumination-invariant-ima/)). Plain watershed on touching leaves scores near zero; marker-controlled watershed with colour-index markers and edge enhancement reaches **74 %** leaf separation, and marker precision is what drives the result ([Abdullah 2018](https://unisep.lib.unishams.edu.my/xmlui/handle/123456789/10995?show=full); [arXiv 1709.06437](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.06437)). Index methods fail in four places: hard shadow, weed-touching-crop (one blob, which only the row model or an instance model can split), senescing weeds that are no longer green, and green residue; the first two are measurable in the app, the last two are unquantified in any source found.

Learned instance models find weeds but draw loose edges, which matters for a product that wants to show a size. In a potato field at 0.27 cm/px, Mask R-CNN and YOLOv8 reached **mAP50 0.92 and 0.90** from only 130 training images, but pixel-level MIoU was **0.515** and the weed class scored 0.82 against potato 0.98 ([bioRxiv 2024.08.13.607729](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.08.13.607729v1.full-text)); in a three-species wheat field F1 stayed at 0.28–0.69 ([preprints.org 202405.0018](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202405.0018)). SAM and SAM 2 do not find weeds zero-shot: on PhenoBench, Grounded-SAM scores **AP50 13.8** and Grounded-SAM 2 **15.9** against a supervised **37.4**, automatic mask generation ranks worst in every agricultural comparison, box prompts beat points, and two positive plus two negative points beat more ([ZeroPlantSeg, arXiv 2509.09116](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09116v1); [Agricultural SAM Adapter](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10534855/); [Frontiers 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/plant-science/articles/10.3389/fpls.2025.1536226/full)). The one agricultural design that uses SAM well treats it as a mask proposer and classes weeds as anomalies relative to the crop's features, with MIT-licensed code ([PRBonn/WeedsAreWeird](https://github.com/PRBonn/WeedsAreWeird)). For Swardus the pattern is therefore: the index blob (or the row model's residual) is the finder, its centroid and box become the prompt, and SAM 2 returns a tighter outline only where the operator or an audit shows the index outline is wrong. That is a model used where it clearly wins, and nowhere else.

What a single near-nadir frame at known scale can measure is projected canopy area, equivalent-circle diameter, major and minor axes and compactness; everything else is a proxy or out of reach. Leaf cover "can be used to approximate leaf area at early stages", and for purple nutsedge the leaf-cover-to-biomass relation was linear regardless of stage, but for older canopies biomass "increased exponentially with ground cover, likely owing to overlapping leaf area" ([Weed Technology operator-assisted module](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/weed-technology/article/validation-of-an-operatorassisted-module-to-measure-weed-and-crop-leaf-cover-by-digital-image-analysis/DFEAADAB76B2B2C0FD4F6BBDBFA8BF74); [Lati et al., Weed Science](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/weed-science/article/abs/robust-methods-for-measurement-of-leafcover-area-and-biomass-from-image-data/C8686BF3ADE502A3F9B3DA41224D32C5); [Agronomy Journal bluestem](https://acsess.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2134/agronj2018.08.0502)). No published area-to-biomass regression exists for Chenopodium, Amaranthus or any arable broadleaf from photographs, so any weight the product showed would be its own uncalibrated guess. Height needs a structure-from-motion surface minus a ground model, and the best reported RMSE is **3.5 cm** for low flights with ground control, **6.8–7.0 cm** for individual cabbage and pumpkin plants, 12–20 cm for canopies at height, with oblique 45° imagery and 40 m beating nadir and 80 m ([PMC5711830](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5711830/); [Agriculture 12(1):102](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-0472/12/1/102); [Drones 8:746](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/drones/drones-08-00746/article_deploy/drones-08-00746.pdf)); a 2–10 cm weed sits inside that error band, so height from nadir survey frames is only a "tall weed above the crop" flag, never a number. Growth stage follows the same logic: leaf counts (BBCH 11–19) need sub-centimetre pixels, a Turkish four-stage classifier found its two later stages "exhibited extreme similarity", and rice staging at 3 m altitude reached about 85 % for three early stages ([ÇOMÜ 2025](https://acikerisim.comu.edu.tr/items/d72da664-16cb-4fa1-bb35-42c8ce157901); [Frontiers 2022 rice](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/plant-science/articles/10.3389/fpls.2022.914771/pdf)). A three-bin stage (seedling, vegetative, flowering-or-seeding) expressed as a BBCH range is defensible; a two-digit code is not. Flowering, by contrast, is visible: RGB hue thresholds mapped leafy-spurge flowering cover at r² 0.64–0.93 against ground estimates and separated yellow-flowering weeds from green crop in alfalfa ([Cambridge Core leafy spurge](https://www-cambridge-org.ezproxyberklee.flo.org/core/product/0B92790C0AB241040421C18CC1A0C0CB/core-reader); [CITA Aragón 2020](https://citarea.cita-aragon.es/bitstream/10532/4976/1/2020_087.pdf)).

Which frame to measure from has a published answer. The INRAE maize-density pipeline selects, per target, frames "with full coverage..., minimum blur and view direction closer to the nadir one", because near-nadir views have "almost no overlap among individual plants" ([Velumani et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.11857)); orthomosaics blend pixels across seams so extracting traits from the original frames is more accurate, and a plot appears in ten or more frames whose index values differ by up to 0.05 ([PMC7609415](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7609415/); [Gray 2023 thesis](https://harvest.usask.ca/items/bcf20e2d-9837-4307-916c-a99f1667ea2e)). No study reports a fusion rule with ground-truth accuracy, so the conservative product rule is the best one or two frames (excluding the frame edge, ranked by the ray's off-nadir angle from the reconstruction's camera pose, then by Laplacian sharpness), the median area where several pass, and the spread shown as the per-plant uncertainty.

## Species naming needs 0.1–0.3 cm pixels; at 1–2 cm the frames give groups, flowers and a date prior

The single most important finding on species is a gap of scale. Every published species-level drone result found was captured at **0.088–0.32 cm/px** from 2–11 m: wheat weeds at 1–6 m scored 88–94 % per species ([Remote Sensing 10:1530](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-10-01530/article_deploy/remotesensing-10-01530.pdf)); soybean weeds scored **94 % at 5 m falling to 79 % at 7.5 m**, and a 15 m model spread from 56 % to 94 % by species with the authors concluding that "altitude-specific models" are needed ([Coscia, Padua thesis](https://thesis.unipd.it/handle/20.500.12608/59118); [Nikolic & Masin 2024](https://research.unipd.it/handle/11577/3573125)); the Spanish DRONEWEED set, which happens to contain Sorghum halepense, Convolvulus arvensis, Portulaca, Chenopodium and Cyperus rotundus, was flown at 11 m for 0.17 cm/px ([DRONEWEED](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11719326/)). Swardus's frames are five to twenty times coarser. What that pixel size supports in the literature is crop versus weed versus soil at 75–94 % overall accuracy (about 75 % at 2.19 cm/px in soybean), a broadleaf-versus-grass split for large patches, and a documented tendency to under-detect grass weeds in visible imagery ([Remote Sensing 2024 soybean GSD](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/16/10/1778); [Castillejo-González / Peña 2014](https://digital.csic.es/handle/10261/160279)). That last point bites hardest in the apricot orchard, where the dangerous perennials are mostly grasses.

Even close-up identification is less certain than vendors imply. Handheld datasets reach 92–96 % on their own test sets ([Weed25](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9748680/); [DeepWeeds](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6375952/)), but Weed25's grasses confused each other and an informal test of Pl@ntNet on 60 weed photos placed the right species first only **60 %** of the time, while eight consumer apps scored **11–67 % fully correct** on weeds at various stages ([UC Weed Science](https://ucanr.edu/node/100003); [AgWeb](https://www.agweb.com/news/crops/crop-production/are-you-spraying-right-weeds-apps-can-help)). Competitors sell a verdict anyway, with Greeneye claiming over 200 species at 95.7 % and Sentera "99 % correlation", and none found exposes a confidence band or confusion matrix to the user ([The Western Producer](https://www.producer.com/crops/greeneye-sees-all-knows-all/); [Iowa State on Sentera](https://crops.extension.iastate.edu/post/drone-based-weed-mapping-targeted-spraying)). WeedScan, the Australian government tool, is the exception worth copying: below 30 % "unreliable", 30–50 % low, 50–80 % moderate, above 80 % high "but reviewing the suggestion is still good practice" ([WeedScan](https://weedscan.org.au/Identify1)).

Three things are available now without a species model. First, the candidate list is short and documented. Turkish orchard surveys report 44–106 species per orchard type but a recurring core: in the Malatya apricot survey the densest were Ranunculus repens and bulbosus, Poa bulbosa and Cynodon dactylon, with Bromus tectorum and Chenopodium album frequent ([Özal University record](https://acikerisim.ozal.edu.tr/items/b535f457-2fd3-439c-a81a-ac8e6252416b)); Antalya citrus had Xanthium strumarium at 64 % frequency, Portulaca oleracea and Sorghum halepense at 60 %, mean cover about 40 % ([Düzce repository](https://acikerisim.duzce.edu.tr/items/6ab50a21-8fd7-4c77-bc07-fb2b831997b6)); Bursa olives were led by Lolium perenne, Chenopodium murale and Sonchus asper ([Namık Kemal repository](https://acikerisim.nku.edu.tr/xmlui/handle/20.500.11776/827)); and Conyza bonariensis, with documented glyphosate resistance, sits in nearly half of surveyed citrus orchards and C. canadensis in a third ([Kaya-Altop et al. 2023](https://acikkaynak.bilecik.edu.tr/items/ea102f08-64f7-4810-841e-bf468b81fe9b/full)). Second, a date prior is light and mathematical: the Muğla citrus survey found **54 species in spring against 29 in autumn** ([Planta Daninha](https://www.scielo.br/j/pd/a/cdzN9HLf8McsXzGynrnKXtw/?lang=en)), and hydrothermal emergence models exist for Conyza bonariensis (base temperature about 10.6 °C, 50 % emergence at about 53 hydrothermal units, field emergence "highly dependent on the rainfall"), for Amaranthus retroflexus and Chenopodium album, and for Lolium rigidum and Avena sterilis ([Zambrano-Navea et al. 2013](https://produccioncientifica.uhu.es/documentos/5e8cecc92999522d380c8037?lang=en); [CNR IRIS](https://iris.cnr.it/handle/20.500.14243/196065); [SJAR review](https://sjar.revistas.csic.es/index.php/sjar/article/download/17876/5245)). Shown as "what is usually up here at this date", with survey sources, this respects the existing catalogue rule of names with sources, never detections. Third, "flowering seen" is often more useful than a name: glyphosate on Conyza works at rosette and fails after bolting in both susceptible and resistant biotypes, and late treatment is blamed for poor control in Turkish citrus ([Kaya-Altop et al. 2023](https://acikkaynak.bilecik.edu.tr/items/ea102f08-64f7-4810-841e-bf468b81fe9b/full); [INTA](https://repositorio.inta.gob.ar/handle/20.500.12123/3504)).

When a name is wanted, the cheap and honest route is the operator's phone, not a lower drone pass. A handheld photo at the flagged spot is the regime where public models actually perform, it avoids the altitude-mismatch failure, and Pl@ntNet is the only ready API with a known commercial price: **500 free queries a day**, then **€1,000 a year for 200,000 requests** plus €5 per thousand over, returning a ranked species with a confidence score and similar-picture URLs, with no warranty of accuracy ([Pl@ntNet terms](https://my.plantnet.org/terms_of_use); [pricing](https://my.plantnet.org/pricing)). iNaturalist data is barred from commercial model training under its current terms as quoted on its forum, and the default photo licence is non-commercial ([iNaturalist forum](https://forum.inaturalist.org/t/can-i-use-inaturalist-aws-open-data-to-train-commercial-ai-model/77663)). For in-house training only DeepWeeds (CC BY 4.0) and Plant Seedlings (CC BY-SA, share-alike) have clear licences, neither contains Turkish orchard species, and the DRONEWEED, Weed25 and CottonWeedDet12 dataset licences could not be read ([DeepWeeds GitHub](https://github.com/AlexOlsen/DeepWeeds); [Aarhus Plant Seedlings](https://vision.eng.au.dk/plant-seedlings-dataset/)). The presentation that fits the evidence is: habit group first (grass, broadleaf rosette, prostrate mat, twining), then a short ranked list of catalogue species consistent with group and date prior, a verbal band rather than a number unless a model validated at this pixel size produced it, the evidence crop beside the proposal, a one-tap verdict (confirm, other, unsure) that is the only thing the map ever shows as a species, and a "needs a close photo" state below the floor. Calibration and abstention rate can then be measured from the operator's own clicks, with no external benchmark ([arXiv 2311.01064](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.01064); [CEUR-WS calibrated weed mapping](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4165/paper1.pdf)).

## Rank by species class, patch at unit scale, zone, stage and history; then export polygons shaped to the machine

No published paper gives a multi-factor patch priority index; what the literature supports is an order of questions an agronomist would recognise, and the product should show the order rather than hide it in weights. Species class comes first because thresholds spread twenty-fold by species: single-year economic thresholds in winter wheat run from **2 plants/m² for Galium aparine to nearly 40 for Bromus sterilis**, with Alopecurus at 23–39 plants/m² in Turkish wheat depending on herbicide cost ([CJAR 2016](https://oes.chileanjar.cl/files/V76i4Y2016id4485.pdf); [Turkish J Agric For 2005](https://aj.tubitak.gov.tr/agriculture/issues/tar-05-29-3/tar-29-3-3-0403-10.pdf)), and in the Spanish maize trial raising the threshold from 6 to 12 plants/m² changed savings by up to 50 % for johnsongrass but only 10–20 % for velvetleaf ([San Martín et al. 2016](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/weed-technology/article/weed-decision-threshold-as-a-key-factor-for-herbicide-reductions-in-sitespecific-weed-management/B89C3B35172948FADCB9CB6B152D3D33)). The Turkish olive guide singles out Sorghum halepense as "çok önemli" for its deep roots, height and role as a virus-vector host ([TAGEM olive guide](https://www.tarimorman.gov.tr/TAGEM/Belgeler/Entegre/Zeytin%20Entegre%20M%C3%BCcadele%20Teknik%20Talimat%C4%B1.pdf)). Since the frames cannot name species, the class must come from the operator's own catalogue verdict, which fits the existing rule; what the product can add is a per-species "treatment difficulty" attribute (rhizomatous perennial, resistance-prone, high seed output) that lifts a patch regardless of its cover.

Second comes cover at the unit scale multiplied by patch area, which is where the new window does its work. Third is zone: in the orchard, inside the 1–1.5 m strip versus in the alley; in row crops, within-row versus inter-row. Fourth is stage and timing. The crop's critical period sets the clock: corn loses about 2 % yield per leaf stage of delay inside a V1–V11 window that narrows to V6–V9 with nitrogen, soybean's window opens at V1 about nine days after emergence, and Turkish cotton's runs **15–60 days after emergence at 70 cm rows** ([UNL CropWatch corn](https://cropwatch.unl.edu/critical-period-weed-control-corn); [Farm Progress soybean](https://www.farmprogress.com/soybean/delayed-soybean-weed-control-costs-3-50-per-day); [Tursun et al. 2016](https://abakus.inonu.edu.tr/items/8469c3fa-1f4a-4523-ad22-fd871de7730f)); the weed's seed set is the hard deadline, since giant foxtail cut at head emergence produced under ten viable seeds against 230–1,200 when heads were out, and velvetleaf set no viable seed when killed at flowering ([MSU Hill et al. 2015](https://www.canr.msu.edu/weeds/research/presented-research/HillEtAlWSSA2015.pdf)). Fifth is history, because perennial patches persist and annual ones move ([Rozenberg et al. 2025](https://ecologylab.net.technion.ac.il/files/2026/05/Gal-Rozenberg-et-al-Pest-Management-Science-2025-Weed-spatial-patterns.pdf); [Izquierdo et al. 2020](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4395/10/4/452)).

The single-plant rule falls out of the same evidence. A lone lambsquarters sets **30,000–176,000 seeds** and a pigweed 13,000–35,000, half of a lambsquarters seedbank survives 12 years, and most seed lands within 2 m ([OSU BYGL](https://bygl.osu.edu/node/2527); [BCPC 1995](https://www.bcpc.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/BCPC-Weeds-1995-Vol-III-Session-8B-and-9.pdf)), so "leave it" is only right if something else (canopy, mowing, cultivation) kills it before flowering. Resistance guidance says the same from the other side: stop seed production, apply labelled rates at recommended weed sizes, scout ([USDA APHIS summary of Norsworthy 2012](https://WWW.APHIS.USDA.GOV/sites/default/files/aphis_bmp_recs_hr_crops.pdf)), and label efficacy drops above roughly 15 cm for grasses and broadleaves ([UNL CropWatch](https://cropwatch.unl.edu/critical-period-weed-control-corn)). So a single plant is a "treat" mark if it is a rhizomatous perennial, a listed high-seed or resistance-prone species by the operator's verdict, or past the label size and still before flowering; otherwise it is a "watch" pin with its measured diameter and a re-check date before flowering. For a young apricot orchard there is one more constraint: the Turkish guide bars non-selective herbicides from orchards whose trunks are not yet woody and lists only glyphosate salts in its annex, preferring shallow tillage, mowing before flowering, cover crops and drip irrigation ([TAGEM olive guide, pp. 56–58](https://www.tarimorman.gov.tr/TAGEM/Belgeler/Entegre/Zeytin%20Entegre%20M%C3%BCcadele%20Teknik%20Talimat%C4%B1.pdf)), so a young-orchard "treat here" mark should default to mechanical wording, which also keeps the product clear of naming a rate.

A ranked list is only useful if it leaves the app in a form a machine or a contractor can act on, and the delivery rules are surprisingly uniform across vendors. DJI Agras controllers take a WGS-84 polygon shapefile for a boundary mission (one flight per polygon, clockwise ring = plot edge, counter-clockwise = obstacle) and a shapefile plus GeoTIFF of rates for a variable-rate mission; Pix4D staff report that **the minimum grid DJI accepts is 1 m**, the drone "needs about 4 to 5 meters to react to a rate change", and the "Max" resample should be chosen because the drone applies the higher of two cells it straddles ([DJI forum](https://forum.dji.com/thread-224202-1-1.html); [PIX4Dfields Agras article](https://support.pix4d.com/hc/en-us/articles/360019956578); [Pix4D community T50 grid](https://community.pix4d.com/t/grid-size-dji-agras-t50/34284)). A T25 trial that mapped on a 0.25 m grid saw the drone demand far more herbicide than the area predicted, for exactly that reason ([Pix4D community T25](https://community.pix4d.com/t/dji-agras-t25-for-spotspraying-trouble-calculatiing-right-amount-of-herbizide-needed/34667)). Spray drones work in zones of roughly **0.1 acre (about 400 m²)**, ground sprayers with nozzle control down to about 4 m² ([Iowa State ICM](https://crops.extension.iastate.edu/post/drone-based-weed-mapping-targeted-spraying)). Ground sprayers take a shapefile with a rate attribute (John Deere, Ag Leader, Agrifac, FieldView's spec: polygons only, EPSG:4326, at most 100 zones) or zipped ISOXML TASKDATA with a grid for any ISOBUS terminal (Amazone Amatron 4, Hardi, Horsch), and on a Deere display "zero rates in the prescription command sections OFF", which is what turns a prescription into a spot map ([FieldView Rx format](https://dev.fieldview.com/rx-format); [Deere display help](https://displaysimulator.deere.com/onscreen_help/4640/current/en/mapping/map_rx.htm); [PIX4Dfields export table](https://support.pix4d.com/hc/en-us/articles/11317942862109)). Every published spot-spray recipe pads: Hohenheim used **1 m × 1 m boxes** around each weed with 0.5 m before and after for nozzle reaction, Amazone extends treatment areas by "a 1 m wide safety zone", Agrovista's drone maps use "typically a minimum of 1m grids", and Sentera reports 7–10 ft minimum spray-zone lengths on most sprayers ([Allmendinger et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11314123/); [Amazone AmaSelect Spot](https://amazone.net/en/service-support/for-the-press/press-releases/press-archive-2019-/amaselect-spot-with-dronelink-45754); [Farm Contractor](https://www.farmcontractormagazine.com/farm-machinery/sprayers/horsch-offers-spot-spraying-without-an-expensive-camera-system.html); [Sentera and Capstan](https://sentera.com/2025/04/20/unlocking-the-power-of-precision-how-sentera-and-capstan-ag-are-transforming-targeted-weed-control/)). The mapping drone and the sprayer should share one RTK correction source or "large location errors are possible" ([PIX4Dfields RTK](https://support.pix4d.com/hc/en-us/articles/36002011123123)). The 0.5 m analysis grid must therefore never be exported raw; the export dilates by 0.5–1 m, merges to polygons, drops or expands anything under the machine's minimum spot, and carries a rate attribute that is an application volume or a 1/0 treat flag with zero meaning sections off, never a product rate.

What growers actually report wanting is the same five artefacts from every service: a colour-coded pressure map, a species list, zones they can edit, a rate-per-zone form, and a download or push to the cab within 24 hours ([Taranis KB](https://knowledge.taranis.ag/portal/en/kb/articles/how-to-create-an-herbicide-prescription-in-the-taranis-web-app); [Sentera SmartScript](https://sentera.com/smartscript-weeds/retail/)). Their complaints with do-it-yourself tools sit at the seams: no built-in buffer, hand-trained classifiers that are "time-consuming", predicted volume that does not match the machine, and a learning curve "a lot of time and hours" long ([Pix4D buffer request](https://community.pix4d.com/t/feature-request-creating-buffers-around-spot-spraying-cells/29272); [Farm Weekly](https://www.farmweekly.com.au/story/9297804/this-graingrowers-strategy-to-improve-crop-health-and-save-money/)). The trustworthy content of a drone map is patch location, not count: a Montana comparison against a camera sprayer found drone ExG missed 41–65 % of individual weeds and YOLO 78–85 % while locating patches well, and UK blackgrass mapping at 3.2 cm/px gave within-field R² of only 0.29–0.46 against observer density bands, with the authors recommending maps as "signposts" for targeted walking ([MSU NARC 2026](https://agresearch.montana.edu/narc/Programs_and_projects/narc-precisionag/newsletterprecisionagupdate/august2026.html); [Lambert et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5832304/)). Grading cover and listing patches, which Swardus already does, is the defensible framing; counts are not. The honest saving to let the operator infer rather than promise is about **47 % at 86 % efficacy in the Hohenheim maize trial and about 46–50 % with 94 % of weeds dead in the Iowa State demonstration**, equal to broadcast and with no yield difference, versus 12 % saving and 20 % efficacy when sprayed late ([Allmendinger et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11314123/); [Iowa State ICM](https://crops.extension.iastate.edu/post/drone-based-weed-mapping-targeted-spraying)); the 80–97 % figures belong to camera sprayers, fallow and vendor case studies. Timing therefore deserves a place on the screen beside the grade: map age, crop stage and days to the next treatment window.

Trust on the operator's own field comes from the imagery, not from a table, which matches the founder's instinct and the literature's unit of validation. Published studies ground-truth with 1 m² quadrats scored by a person and report kappa and a cover regression (r² 0.80 overall, 0.91 for Palmer amaranth in cotton) ([AgriEngineering 2020](https://www.mdpi.com/2624-7402/2/2/24)); Hohenheim validated by marking weeds by hand on the same imagery; products earn belief by showing the photo beside the mark, by a close-up confirmation flight, and by a re-fly after the weeds die "to verify the spray worked" ([Drone Ag wiki](https://droneag.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SS/pages/1650884609); [Corteva](https://www.corteva.com/us/Resources/crop-protection/soybeans/drones-valuable-weed-control-tool.html)). Drone indices correlate only 0.64 with visual efficacy ratings, best at 7 and 14 days after application, so the before-and-after pair of frames is the decisive check, not a new metric ([Iowa State efficacy study](https://dr.lib.iastate.edu/entities/publication/1a5a038b-0c44-4334-94fa-8f0c80d6f0c8)). Because models transfer poorly between fields (Lambert's field-to-field correlation was 0.34), a per-field audit is required work rather than polish. Patch memory is worth more in the orchard than in the row crops: perennials "demonstrating persistent patches" was the main finding of a two-year Israeli study, grass patches were more stable than ragweed over ten years in a US field, and Catalan cereal patches had to be "identified afresh in each season" except for Lolium and Bromus ([Rozenberg et al. 2025](https://ecologylab.net.technion.ac.il/files/2026/05/Gal-Rozenberg-et-al-Pest-Management-Science-2025-Weed-spatial-patterns.pdf); [Clay et al., SDSU](https://openprairie.sdstate.edu/plant_faculty_pubs/162); [Izquierdo et al. 2020](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4395/10/4/452)). Stored patch outlines tagged with the operator's own perennial-or-annual verdict are a prior for next season's walk and a pre-emergence target, not a prescription to reuse blind; and an offline phone with cached tiles, the patch list and the source frames covers the low-connectivity user that closed services like Skippy Scout (needs in-field internet) and Taranis (offline only inside its service) do not ([Drone Ag wiki](https://droneag.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SS/pages/1650884609); [Taranis offline scouting](https://knowledge.taranis.ag/portal/en/kb/articles/offline-scoutin)).

## Conclusion

The research reframes the problem Swardus set out with. "Treat here" was never going to come from better detection at 0.5 m; it comes from applying the thresholds at the scale they were measured and the machine can execute, and from letting single plants earn treatment by biology (perennial, large, flowering) rather than by cover. The same reframing settles the measurement question: the frames are good for one number per weed (area, hence diameter), one flag (flowering), and one bin (stage), and that is more than enough to run the single-plant rule and the priority order, while species, height and biomass are either out of reach at 1–2 cm/px or belong to a handheld photo and the operator's verdict. The founder's preference for light mathematical methods is not a compromise here; it is what the evidence supports, with one model (SAM 2 as an Apache-licensed outline refiner) invited only where the index outline is visibly wrong.

The commercial implication is that the gap between Swardus and the service vendors is not detection quality but the last two screens: editable zones that export as machine-shaped, buffered polygons with treated hectares and no rate, and an in-app audit and re-scan that show agreement on the operator's own imagery. Every vendor sells a verdict and hides its uncertainty; a product that shows the unit it decided on, the plant it measured, the reason it ranked a patch first, and the frames before and after treatment is differentiated precisely by being checkable, and in a young Turkish orchard, where the floor is perennial and the trunks are not yet woody, that checkability plus mechanical-first wording is also what the national IPM guidance asks for.

## Recommended next steps, in order

1. **Decide in a treatment-unit window, draw on the 0.5 m grid.** Compute cover in a moving window equal to the treatment unit (orchard: the planting layout's tree-strip band, about 1–1.5 m either side of the trunk line, in 2 m lengths; row crops: nozzle-section width × 2 m along the row, operator-adjustable, capped at 6 × 6 m), stepped at 0.5 m, and apply the existing crop-group thresholds to the window. The operator sees the same grid, but red cells now outline patches a sprayer can hit; a lone 20 cm plant that used to be a red cell becomes a small pin, and a "Treatment unit" control on the Weed pressure panel lets him drag the unit size and watch the map settle. Alley cells in the orchard show their cover but are not marked "treat here" unless he declares a bare floor.

2. **Measure each kept spot on its best source frame.** For every spot, pick the covering frame nearest nadir and sharpest (not at the frame edge), segment with ExGR + Otsu under a shadow mask, and report projected canopy area, equivalent diameter, the spread across the frames that qualify, and a flowering flag from yellow/white hue; bin stage as seedling, vegetative or flowering-or-seeding with a BBCH range. The operator sees a spot card with the native-pixel crop, the outline drawn on it, "about 18 cm across (16–21 cm across 3 photos)", "flowering seen" when it applies, and never a weight or a height.

3. **Turn single plants into "watch" or "treat" by rule, with the reason shown.** A lone plant is "treat" only if its catalogue verdict is a rhizomatous perennial or resistance-prone species, or it is past the label size and not yet flowering; otherwise it is "watch" with a re-check date before the species' flowering window. The operator sees the pin coloured by that outcome and one plain line under it, such as "watch: 12 cm, no verdict, re-check by 15 May" or "treat: operator marked Sorghum halepense".

4. **Rank patches as treat first, treat next, watch, in the order species class, cover × area at unit scale, strip or alley, stage, seen last scan.** The operator sees a ranked list beside the map, each row with its hectares, its reason line in that order, and a tap that flies the map to it; young-orchard rows carry mechanical wording (hoe, mow before flowering) by default.

5. **Add a random cell audit.** Draw N random cells per class, show the native source-frame crop beside the grade, record agree or disagree, and keep a running "of the cells you checked, X of Y agreed" per class with the date; let two or three operator-marked patches seed the field read. The operator sees an Audit tab with the frame and the grade side by side, and an agreement line per colour on the legend.

6. **Export machine-ready treatment maps.** Dilate decided cells by a chosen 0.5–1 m buffer, merge to polygons, drop or expand spots under the machine's minimum (1 × 1 m for nozzle-control booms, longer for section booms, about 400 m² zones for spray drones), and write a WGS-84 shapefile with a treat flag or application-volume attribute (zero = sections off), a GeoTIFF + TFW on a 1 m grid with Max resample for DJI/XAG VRA missions, and zipped ISOXML for ISOBUS terminals, with the field boundary and obstacles as separate rings. The operator sees an Export panel: machine picker, buffer slider, minimum spot, treated area in hectares of the field's total, map age, and a warning that the controller's own total will differ and that the sprayer should use the same RTK source as the survey.

7. **Re-scan comparison and patch memory.** After a treatment, pair the same cells from the 7–14-day re-scan and draw the grade difference; store patch outlines per season with the operator's perennial-or-annual verdict. The operator sees a Before/After toggle on the map, a "dead / still green" tally for the treated patches, and next season a faint "last year's patches" layer for the field walk and for widening a pre-emergence target, all available offline with cached tiles and frames.

8. **Species proposal via the operator's phone, not the drone.** Show the habit group the frame supports (grass, rosette, mat, twining), then the catalogue species consistent with that group and a date-and-region prior from the Turkish surveys and hydrothermal emergence windows, each with its source and a verbal confidence band; offer "take a close photo" that sends the handheld image to Pl@ntNet (500 free queries a day, €1,000 a year beyond) and shows its ranked species with similar-picture evidence. The operator sees a proposal, never a verdict; only his confirm/other/unsure becomes the map's species label, and the confirm rate per band is shown back to him as calibration.

9. **Models and licences, only where they clearly win.** Try SAM 2 (Apache-2.0) as an outline refiner prompted by the index blob's box plus two positive and two negative points, judged only on the audit screen against the index outline; run CampanetaWeeds2 (CC BY 4.0) as the first external orchard sanity check after opening it to confirm GSD and mask type; keep ERFNet and PhenoBench research-only until licences are settled; skip YOLOv8 (AGPL) and iNaturalist (non-commercial); and if height ever matters, plan a dedicated low oblique flight rather than mining nadir frames. The operator sees nothing new until a refined outline visibly beats the index outline on his own spots, which is the bar for keeping it.
